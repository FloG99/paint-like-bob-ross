# S19E13 Valley of Tranquility
- palette: phthalo blue, titanium white, bright red (tiny), black, Prussian blue, Van Dyke brown, alizarin crimson, phthalo green, cadmium yellow, sap green, yellow ochre, Indian yellow, dark sienna
- canvas: portrait (~3:4, ~18x24 (?)). Thin even coat of Liquid White over everything, wet and slick. Nothing pre-painted. Sky, mountains and lake are all worked into the wet white.
- light: from the right (snow on right-facing slopes, left faces of the big mountain in shadow). Bright cool daylight, cumulus sky, misty valley, summer green meadow. Series finale, billed as a lovely little mountain scene.
- note: the public reference image of the finished painting is a 4:3 image of what is really a portrait canvas (video 26:46), so it looks horizontally stretched/cropped and shows little lake. Fractions below come from video frames; vertical fractions are of canvas height (0 = top).

## Steps
### 00:30 Sky: blue shadow bands
- tool: 1-inch brush, corner/tip of the bristles.
- load: phthalo blue only, just a little; reloaded per band (tint, little more of the color [01:12]).
- motion: continuous wiggle/zigzag, a wavy ribbon 4-6 in long (?), ~0.5 in amplitude, light pressure, drawn left-to-right and right-to-left. Each band is a fuzzy blue caterpillar.
- placement: 3-4 separate bands in the upper ~50% of the canvas, running roughly across with a slight rise to the right: top band left edge ~0.12 high to right ~0.07 (00:44-00:58); a short bit above-left ~0.1 (01:05); a lower band from the left ~0.25 to the right ~0.15 (00:51-01:12); another lower right at ~0.3 (01:20, 01:30). Bands are well separated with white between them.
- result: blue squiggles on white. Only the white between matters: he is focused on the white area, not the blue [00:46].
- seen: 00:44, 00:51, 01:05, 01:12, 01:20, 01:30

### 01:28 Soften the blue
- tool: second clean, dry 1-inch brush; no paint added.
- motion: drag the blue back and down into the liquid white with short back-and-forth wiggles (working it in and blending it [01:55]), working from the lower side of each band. The top edge of each band stays defined; the blue fades downward into pale blue-white. He stays off the white edge for now (he is avoiding it at the moment [01:37]).
- result: each band is a defined blue top with a soft fade running down (01:55-02:12). Softness is a choice (the other option is leaving it fairly dark [02:08]).
- rule: the more you blend, the softer and paler it gets [01:45]
- seen: 01:38, 01:47, 01:55, 02:04, 02:12

### 02:37 Cumulus: white edges
- tool: 1-inch brush (washed after step 2), corner of the bristles only [02:54].
- load: titanium white + the smallest possible touch of bright red for a pinkish glow [02:32]; well loaded.
- motion: let that brush move freely around: tiny scrubbing circles / taps with the corner, light-to-medium pressure, building a lumpy cauliflower edge of ~0.5-1 in bumps. Works along the underside of each blue band, laying white over the soft blue fade and bumping upward into the blue; moves left to right and back. Farthest (top) cloud first.
- placement: along the lower edge of each blue band, from the top of the canvas down to ~0.55 height; bumps point up into the blue.
- result: crisp scrubby white puffs with blue shadow pockets between them (02:50-03:22); white and blue still look separate.
- seen: 02:50, 03:01, 03:12, 03:22

### 03:26 Cumulus: blend
- tool: 2-inch brush, corner only; chosen for its fuller bristle body, which blends nicer [03:42].
- motion: touch where white meets blue and sweep in small arcs following the cloud contour, light pressure. Then a barely-there lift and fluff [03:50]: a nearly weightless pass across the tops of the white bumps, lifting off at the end. The bumpy outer silhouette is kept; only the white/blue joint melts.
- result: soft, big fluffy clouds, blue shadow fading into white tops (03:44, 03:55).
- seen: 03:33, 03:44, 03:55

### 04:03 Lower cloud (nearest)
- tool: 1-inch for blue and white, 2-inch for blend and fluff.
- motion: same sequence: blue squiggle (a bit of blue down in this spot [04:25]), blend, white+red corner dabs on the underside, large-brush blend and fluff [04:36-04:46].
- placement: below the first clouds, ~0.45-0.6 height, in front of them; the white mass hangs as a bumpy cloud with the lightest pink-white bottom.
- result: layered sky, top ~55% covered, blue only in the shadow pockets (04:55). Rule: work from farthest cloud to nearest.
- rule: he tends to paint the farthest cloud first, then move forward [04:12]
- seen: 04:05, 04:15, 04:25, 04:35, 04:45, 04:55

### 05:04 Far mountain
- tool: palette knife (large), edge; then 2-inch brush.
- load: black + Prussian blue + Van Dyke + a little crimson mixed on the palette; cut a small roll of paint and load it on the knife edge [05:17-05:21]. Only a very small amount of paint is used.
- motion: knife nearly flat, edge down; apply strong force, pressing paint into the fabric [05:37] along the ridge, drawing one small peak with two gently sloping sides (~15-20° from horizontal) and dragging outward from the peak. Then the 2-inch brush grabs and pulls it sideways and blends so it drifts about in the sky [05:49]: broad horizontal sweeps, lifting off at the ends (05:50-06:10).
- placement: right of center, peak at ~0.55 width, ~0.37 height (?); after blending it spreads to a long low ridge ~0.8 of the width, bottom lost in the pale cloud base.
- result: grey-violet ghost mountain. Optional highlight: a bit of white on the knife, dab on the ridge [06:04], then 2-inch brush taps it soft, suggesting a small highlight without really adding much [06:32].
- rule: it is too distant to carry much detail [05:53]
- seen: 05:40, 05:50, 06:00, 06:10, 06:20, 06:30

### 06:49 Big mountain (left)
- tool: knife; then 2-inch brush flat.
- load: same dark mix as 05:04, a thin rolled strip along the blade edge, reloaded.
- motion: start at the upper-left summit (~0.12 width, ~0.22 height) and pull the knife down and to the right along a long diagonal (steeper at first, ~35-45°, then shallower), pressing hard. Jog the blade up/down along the way to cut bumps and steps (perhaps a small bump is there [07:08]). Then fill under the ridge with short 1-2 in side strokes along the same diagonal, textured and ragged at the lower edge. Ridge ends at ~0.8 width, ~0.5 height (07:25-07:49); by 08:25 there is a second shoulder at ~0.4 width/0.4 height.
- placement: left half to center; left side runs off canvas edge; summit at ~0.22 height, bigger and nearer than the far mountain, overlapping its left base.
- result: dark violet wedge with a harsh stepped ridge and ragged lower edge (07:49).
- rule: press the paint firmly into the fabric and be forceful with it [07:12]
- seen: 06:50, 07:01, 07:13, 07:25, 07:37, 07:49, 08:01

### 07:33 Scrape and mist
- tool: 2-inch brush, flat, brush at ~vertical to the canvas surface of strokes.
- motion: firm downward pulls from the ridge (scraping with firmness [07:33]), spreading slightly at the bottom, then short light horizontal sweeps at the base (08:13-08:25): removes excess paint and blends the bottom until it fades away completely [08:00]. Pressure firm at the top, light at the base.
- result: crisp dark top, lower ~50% fades to lavender mist (08:25, 08:30).
- rule: more defined at the top than at the bottom, since mist is present lower down [08:05]
- rule: scraping excess makes the next layer of paint adhere far more readily [07:42]
- seen: 07:49, 08:01, 08:13, 08:25, 08:30

### 08:34 Lake
- tool: same 2-inch brush, wiped (not washed).
- load: phthalo blue + small phthalo green + a little black, mixed on the palette and picked up on the brush (08:46, 08:54).
- motion: flat horizontal side-to-side strokes across the width, brush flat, medium pressure; begins at the left edge (09:02) and fills to the right (09:10). Brush then washed vigorously [09:50].
- placement: from ~0.55 height down to the bottom; the top edge blends up into the mountain mist; nothing is left painted over later except trees and bank.
- result: smooth aqua-blue band with fine horizontal brush streaks (09:10, 09:55).
- seen: 08:38, 08:46, 08:54, 09:02, 09:10, 09:55

### 09:55 Snow on the mountains (knife)
- tool: palette knife, long edge; a slim cylinder of paint along the blade edge [10:08].
- load: titanium white pulled out flat on the palette, a thin roll cut off the edge [10:01].
- motion: touch the canvas almost imperceptibly with no pressure [10:13-10:16]; blade low and almost flat, only the edge skims the surface, dragged along the slope direction in many short strokes (~1-3 in), each starting at the ridge and skidding down; the white catches the canvas tooth and breaks into speckle. Strokes lie roughly in the slope's direction (down-right, ~20-35° below horizontal on the middle hill; steeper, ~50-60°, on the big mountain's long right face (?)). Repeated close together at the ridge, sparser further down.
- placement: starts on the middle hill (~0.45 width, ~0.4 height) right-facing slope (10:19-10:35), then the big left mountain's right-facing face from the summit (~0.12, 0.22) down toward the right (10:43-11:55); the far mountain got only its small highlight. Left (shadowed) faces stay dark. Snow thins out into flecks and fades about two thirds down the slope.
- result: broken sparkly snow with vertical stipple and gully streaks, ridge crest brightest (11:00, 11:55, 12:58).
- rule: the angles matter most; most of them should run in one common direction [12:00-12:07]
- rule: highlights should share one angle, and shadows a similar basic angle [12:27]
- seen: 10:19, 10:22, 10:27, 10:30, 10:35, 10:43, 10:51, 11:00, 11:06, 11:13, 11:20, 11:27, 11:34, 11:41, 11:48, 11:55

### 12:42 Mountain shadows

- placement: under ridge steps and on the shadow side of each snow rib, mostly on the big mountain's upper half and on the middle hill's right face (12:51-13:47); the middle hill ends with a blue-grey cap over a darker core.

## Rules and principles he states
- Blending blue into liquid white: the longer you blend, the paler and softer the result [01:45]
- Farthest cloud first, then forward [04:12]
- Big brush for blending cumulus, corner only [03:38]
- Far mountain: little paint, no detail [05:53]
- Mountain edges: push paint into the fabric [07:12]; scrape excess so the next layer sticks [07:42]
- Mountains crisper at top, misty at base [08:05]
- Keep mountain angles consistent: highlights one direction, shadows the opposing one [12:05-12:32]
- Shadow under a protrusion makes it pop [13:10]
- Mix color on the brush for variety [14:39]
- Fill treeline densely or it looks like a fence [17:56]
- Rock in front of a waterfall pushes it back [21:32]
- Do not kill all the dark of an evergreen [25:11]
- Work in layers, back to front [26:03]; fortunate accidents happen [09:08]

## Finished painting
- focal point: the snow ridge of the big mountain (~0.3, 0.3) with the waterfall bank (~0.62, 0.65); the big dark evergreen at the right edge (~0.9, 0.5) frames the scene.
- planes: far = pale violet peak (~0.55, 0.37) in clouds; middle = big snowy mountain plus middle hill with misty base, grass wedge, tiny distant trees; mid-near = dense treeline, meadow bank with rocks and waterfall; near = lake with reflections, bushes at lower right, big evergreen at right.
- darkest darks: evergreens at right, treeline base, dark bank under the meadow; lightest lights: snow crest and cloud tops.
- proportions: sky and clouds ~35%, mountains + grass ~25%, treeline/bank ~8%, lake ~30% (?).
- light from the right; cool blues/violets above, warm yellow-greens in the meadow.
- depth: far peak soft and violet, big mountain crisp at top and faded to mist at base, treeline blue-dark with soft bottoms, near bank sharp with warm greens and crisp rocks.

## Surprises
- Clouds: blue is painted first as wiggly ribbons, softened, and the cloud shapes are the white dabs added on top of the blue's lower side; the white corner dabs make the bumps, the big brush only blends.
- The lake is painted right after the mountains and before the snow, grass and trees; the grass wedge and treeline are then laid over it.
- Snow is a whisper-light knife drag with almost no pressure on a dark base; the broken tooth gives the texture.
- A fall about an inch tall; it is set back by a rock painted in front of it.
