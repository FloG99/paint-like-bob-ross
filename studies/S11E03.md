# S11E03 Daisy Delight
- palette: dark sienna, alizarin crimson, Van Dyke brown, phthalo blue, midnight black, sap green, cadmium yellow, yellow ochre, titanium white (+ liquid clear as base/thinner, odorless thinner)
- canvas: 18x24 PORTRAIT. Whole canvas covered with a very thin coat of Liquid Clear (not white); a tiny amount, it goes a long way. Nothing pre-painted. Colors stay transparent on it and the dried clear areas turn shiny like a glaze.
- light: no sun shown. Warm brown/tan glow behind the trees, strongest upper middle and right (x0.5-0.95, y0.1-0.55); cool dark blue-green trees, pale grey-tan ground. Calm, hazy, undefined season.

## Steps
### 1:08 Abstract background
- tool: flat brush, size not shown (1-2in ?)
- load: a tiny amount of one color at a time, dark sienna, alizarin, Van Dyke, a touch of phthalo; back and forth between them
- motion: short vertical streaks and dabs, going top to bottom; colors meet but are NOT fully blended
- placement: upper two thirds, brown in the upper band (y0-0.55), streaks reach down to ~y0.6 at left and right; lower part stays pale
- result: transparent brown-red streaked glow on grey-clear (frames 2:55, 3:58)
- rule: transparent colors on clear give a different look than on white [1:40]; no detail here [3:47]
- seen: 1:21, 1:52, 2:24, 2:55, 3:58

### 3:10 Far trees (blur)
- tool: same brush, tip/face tapped
- load: dark sienna, pick up a little crimson, later Van Dyke for darker ones
- motion: tap, tiny; some tapped darker
- placement: right of center, upper area (about x0.55-0.8, y0.15-0.4), a few smaller elsewhere
- result: dark fuzzy smudge shapes (frame 4:30, dark cluster at x~0.7, y~0.2-0.35)
- seen: 3:27, 4:30

### 4:40 Soften
- tool: same brush, wiped, lightly beaten
- motion: barely touching, lift and blend the dabs back into the ground
- seen: 5:00

### 5:00 Speckle (thinner on clear)
- tool: fan brush dipped in thinner; a knife edge flicks the thinner off
- motion: tiny flicks from the knife edge onto the canvas, few at a time; more can be added later
- result: lots of small round blotches and pale cells in the brown, grow larger the longer they sit; reads as masses of distant leaves
- rule: thinner and clear react strongly with each other [5:10]; keep it to a little at a time [5:30]
- seen: 5:24, 5:36, 7:54, 10:18

### 6:36 Big evergreens (first)
- tool: 2in brush, sharp chisel edge
- load: blue + black + brown + sap green + crimson, very dark, plenty. Pull the brush through the paint with a wiggle on both sides, then shape it to a knife-like edge. Little paint = bristles flop open
- motion: corner of the brush, work back and forth, pushing downward; each pass a little wider as it descends; tree built tier by tier
- placement: first tree just left of center (x~0.4, top y~0.08, foot y~0.6), about 0.2 wide at the bottom
- result: tall detailed dark blue-green spruce (frames 7:54, 8:26)
- rule: nearer trees get more detail [7:37]
- seen: 6:50, 7:22, 7:54, 8:26

### 8:08 Back plane and sticks
- tool: round brush, then liner with thinned paint
- load: tiny dark sienna, transparent
- motion: tap bushes; liner adds a handful of short sticks and twigs
- placement: low on the right of the tree, x0.65-0.9, y0.4-0.6 (brown bushes in frame 8:58)
- rule: several planes build depth [7:56]; a plane at the back pushes the rest forward [8:04]
- seen: 8:58

### 9:23 Far tree rows
- tool: 2in, loaded full, sharp chisel
- motion: tap down and give a small pull; makes row after row of thin trees; spaced wherever
- placement: lower band of the tree area, left half and under the big tree, tops y~0.5-0.62
- result: jagged fringe of small spruce along the base (frame 10:18)
- seen: 9:40, 10:18

### 10:50 More evergreens
- tool: 2in chisel, reshaped sharp between each tree
- motion: corner, push down, one tree at a time; different heights, one small one fitted in
- placement: next to the first, group spreads to x0.05-0.65, tallest y0.05; trees overlap
- result: dense cluster of dark spruce left of center with clear sky at right (frames 11:34, 12:50)
- rule: reload and re-sharpen between trees for best result [11:17]
- seen: 10:56, 11:34, 12:50

### 12:02 Trunks and twigs
- tool: knife; roll of dark sienna + least white cut off from the pile
- motion: drop short trunk pieces here and there; scrape the knife through wet paint for sticks
- placement: just a few places on the trees, never a full trunk
- seen: 12:12

### 12:55 Highlights on trees
- tool: same dirty 2in, chisel edge again, corner only
- load: cad yellow worked into the leftover blue-black = dark green
- motion: gentle corner taps on the tips of branches; a hint of thinner makes thin paint stick to thick
- placement: upper right sides of the trees
- result: tree tips turn green, rest stays dark (frames 14:12, 14:36)
- rule: too many highlights kill the dark and flatten [14:04]
- seen: 13:00, 13:24, 13:48, 14:12

### 14:18 Lay of the land
- tool: a second flat brush (2in ?)
- load: almost no paint, dark taken from the tree base itself
- motion: scrub it in, then tap downward and let it spread outward; angle changed on the right to make a small rise
- placement: from tree foot (y~0.6) spreading down ~0.15 to y~0.75, a gentle slope up at the right
- result: soft teal haze under the trees, brown below it (frames 16:56, 17:28)
- rule: this is how every plane is made [16:00]; more tapping = softer [16:43]
- seen: 15:00, 15:52, 16:24, 16:56, 17:28, 18:00

### 18:00 Fence
- tool: edge of a small knife
- motion: press down very hard, pull straight down; posts first (two or three), then horizontal rails with the same edge, edge pressed so hard the blade bends
- placement: two groups at the tree foot: left small ~x0.2-0.4, right bigger ~x0.55-0.8, y~0.58-0.64; the nearer posts and rails drawn wider
- result: thin pale-scratched posts and rails showing through the dark (frames 19:06, 20:02)
- rule: wider as it gets closer pushes the rest back [19:15]
- seen: 19:06, 20:02, 20:30

### 19:37 Fence highlight and base
- tool: liner, white + least brown; then big brush
- motion: thin line along the tops of rails; then tap the fence bases with the big brush, picking up dark, for grass at their feet

### 20:40 Foreground grass mound
- tool: fan brush
- load: Van Dyke + dark sienna, dipped in liquid clear so it turns thin and shiny; later yellow ochre
- motion: punch the brush in with hard pressure so the bristles bend upward; then with the corner pull up blades, curving and wiggling, not all straight; ochre blades lighter, lighter away from the dark core (the core stays dark, more shadow)
- placement: one crescent hump low center-left, x0.1-0.68, y0.68-0.86, crest highest left of middle (frames 21:10-22:50)
- result: shaggy dark brown mound with ochre tips, soft edges later
- seen: 21:00, 21:10, 21:21, 21:32, 21:58, 22:24, 22:50

### 22:26 Soften mound base
- tool: large brush, tap
- motion: tapped at the base, picks up mound color and moves outward; smooths the base and makes another plane

### 23:00 White daisies
- tool: filbert no. 6 (liner could also do it)
- load: titanium white dipped into clear until quite thin; kept thin all the way through, rewet when it thickens
- motion: pop the brush on, a very short press-and-flick for each petal, outward from a shared center; about five or six petals (?) in a ring, loose; no outline, the flower can tilt or look sideways
- size: flower head ~0.04-0.05 of canvas width across (?), petal ~0.015-0.02 long (?)
- placement: along the top crest of the mound and a few in front of it above the lawn: first one ~x0.6, y0.64 (frame 23:26), then x0.3-0.65, y0.58-0.78; clustered unevenly, not in rows; a couple on the pale ground right of the mound
- result: small white flowers with grey-blue shading, paint pulled a little downward below some of them as a pale wedge (?) (frames 24:33, 24:40)
- rule: keep the paint very thin [23:55]; as many or as few as you like [23:36]
- seen: 23:26, 23:50, 24:00, 24:20, 24:33, 24:40

### 24:24 Blue daisies
- tool: same filbert, wiped
- load: phthalo, thin on clear, reads pale blue on the canvas so mixed with some white or just very thin (?)
- motion: same pop; fewer than white
- placement: mixed in among the white ones, more at lower left and inside the mound (x0.2-0.45, y0.75-0.85), one left of the mound at ~x0.1, y0.7 (frames 25:16, 26:10)
- seen: 24:58, 25:16, 26:10

### 25:50 Centers
- tool: filbert, wiped, a little clear
- load: yellow ochre, small amount
- motion: touch once into the middle of a daisy; a dab of ~0.01 canvas width (?); not every flower needs one
- result: warm yellow-brown dot at the center (frames 26:10, 26:23)
- seen: 25:52, 26:10, 26:23

### 26:10 Stems and twigs
- tool: liner dipped in thinner
- load: brown
- motion: quick short upward strokes from the mound; short straight or slightly bent stems under some flowers; also thin grass blades and a few sticks
- placement: in and just above the mound, mostly under daisies near the crest (frames 26:30, 26:37)
- rule: a few stems keep the flowers from looking dropped [26:31]
- seen: 26:16, 26:30, 26:37

## Rules and principles he states
- Clear coat: the tiniest amount; paint colors go on very little [0:55; 1:15].
- Transparent colors on clear give a different, soft look [1:40].
- Keep the background abstract, no detail [3:47].
- Thinner on clear reacts and makes blotches; add a little at a time, more can be added [5:10-5:35].
- Close trees get detail; shaping the brush edge sharp and loading it full keeps the bristles together [7:00-7:40].
- Many planes make depth; a plane at the back pushes things forward [7:56].
- Highlights on the dark trees few, or the dark is lost [14:04]; thin paint sticks to thick [13:38].
- Reload and re-sharpen brush between trees [11:17].
- Planes made by tapping dark outward with almost no paint; angle controls the lay [16:00; 16:35].
- Fences get wider closer to the viewer [19:00; 19:15].
- Mound: lighter strokes farther from the dark core [22:15].
- Daisy paint kept thin; clear leaves a gloss glaze when dry [23:55-24:11].
- Add stems so the flowers hold up [26:31].

## Finished painting
- focal point: the dark spruce group x0.05-0.65, y0.05-0.6, and below it the daisy mound at about (0.4, 0.75)
- planes: far = brown streaked haze and fuzzy distant trees (upper); middle = spruce, back bushes at right, fence at tree foot (y0.58-0.64); near = grass mound with daisies, bare tan ground in front (y0.86-1.0)
- darkest darks: spruce group center and the core of the mound; lightest lights: white daisies, fence top, sky glow upper right
- framing: trees on left, open glowing sky at right; the mound sits under the trees slightly left
- proportions: trees/background ~60%, mid ground ~15%, foreground ~25%
- palette temperature: warm brown background, cool blue-green trees, warm ochre mound, blue and white flowers

## Surprises
- Clear coat instead of white; transparent paint, abstract background first with no planned scene.
- Thinner flicked on wet paint on purpose to get blotchy texture.
- Spruce are made with the big 2in brush chisel edge; far trees by a tap-and-pull.
- Land is made by pulling dark out of the tree base with almost no paint, not by a new color.
- Fence cut in with a knife edge pressed hard, no paint load.
- Daisies done at the very end on a thin mix with a filbert; yellow centers added afterwards; some flowers blue; stems last with the liner.
