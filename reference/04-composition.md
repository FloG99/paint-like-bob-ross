# Composition

Measured on the finished paintings of the studied episodes (fractions of the canvas, x from
the left, y from the top).

## The standard layout (landscape canvas)
| band | y | contents |
|---|---|---|
| sky | 0–0.35/0.45 | darkest at the top corners, light at the horizon; cumulus in the upper half |
| far plane | 0.1–0.55 | the focal mountain: summit at x 0.3–0.5, y 0.08–0.35; ranges overlap, each nearer one lower and darker |
| mist | at the mountain base | a pale band where the far plane dissolves |
| middle plane | 0.5–0.7 | tree line or far shore on a near-horizontal line, the water's far edge |
| near plane | 0.65–1.0 | water or stream in the center, banks left and right, big dark trees at the edges |

Typical shares: sky 35–45%, mountains 15–30%, water 25–40% (lakes) or 10–25% (streams).

## Rules seen in every mountain and water study
- **Focal point**: a lit snowy peak slightly off center (x 0.3–0.5). With a waterfall, the falls and the peak stack vertically. The lightest lights sit there. [S11E01, S12E02, S17E07]
- **Framing darks**: a big dark evergreen or tree mass at one edge, often running off the top, sometimes both edges. Dark trees at the edges frame the scene and push everything else back. [S07E04 17:47]
- **Open center**: the middle of the near plane stays open (water, sheen, stream, path), so the eye goes in.
- **Leading light**: water or a path enters from the bottom and narrows toward the focal point, widening toward the viewer. [S12E13 23:54, S11E01]
- **Planes and mist**: three or more planes, each with a soft base and a sharper top. Little hills or rocks are added as extra planes to push things back. [S20E09 13:42, S08E06 15:40]
- **Overlap for depth**: a rock in front of a fall, bushes on both sides of a path, a tree over the mountain's foot. [S19E13 21:32, S20E09 26:41]
- **Light from one side**, usually the right, for every element. [S20E09 09:52]
- **Darkest darks near the edges and the foreground**, the lightest lights at the focal point and on the water.
- **Odd, varied groups**: one big tree with a smaller companion tree, trees of different heights, never a fence. [S19E13 17:56]

## In the six studies of thinner subjects (ocean, barn, night, moon, flowers, desert)
- **Focal point follows the subject**: the wave's crash (0.8, 0.48), a large central barn (peak 0.61, 0.43), a peak under the aurora (0.28, 0.27), the moon, a spruce group over daisies, a red rock. Darkest and lightest still meet there. [S10E10, S07E07, S08E13, S01E06, S11E03, S21E11]
- **Back light** in three (S10E10, S07E07, S21E11): forms read as silhouettes with lit rims, not as lit sides. Night scenes are lit by the moon or aurora only.
- **Framing darks hold**: trees at both edges at night, a bare tree and a tree mass around the barn. [S08E13, S01E06, S07E07]

## Format variants
- **Portrait**: falls or mountain stacked over a pool. Sky about 10–35%, with the focal point in the middle third. [S17E07, S19E13] A tall tree group can fill the upper 60% instead, with the open glow on one side and the foreground mound below. [S11E03]
- **Oval mask**: contact paper is cut out and removed late, and a big tree is painted across the oval's edge. [S20E09] Two studied episodes, both portrait:
  1. **Mask**: contact paper with an oval cut out, about x 0.10–0.88, y 0.09–0.92 of the canvas; Liquid White over everything. [S20E09 00:45, S21E11 01:07]
  2. **Inside**: paint as usual; horizon just below the oval's middle (stated), the sky an egg-shaped glow, the ground darker toward the rim. [S21E11 03:18, 11:42, S20E09 02:20]
  3. **Peel late**, at about 0.85 of the episode, leaving a hard edge on bare canvas. [S21E11 22:19, S20E09 22:44]
  4. **Break the edge** with one near subject on the side opposite the main mass: a big tree pushed up from the lower right [S20E09 22:52], a cactus at the left with ground at its foot so it does not hang [S21E11 22:50, 23:46]. Something crosses the edge in 10 of the 13 episodes whose narration names the oval. [S12E12 24:27, S15E05 25:03]

## Element frequencies (all 403 episodes)
Trees 90%, clouds 44%, mountains 40%, lakes 35%, rivers 31%. When a mountain is present it comes with conifers 81%, a snowy peak 68% and a lake 51%. When a river is present it comes with deciduous trees 66%, grass 48%, rocks 29% and a waterfall 27%. See `dataset/stats.md`.
