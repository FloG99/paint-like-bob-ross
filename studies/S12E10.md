# S12E10 Mountain at Sunset
- palette: Liquid Clear (base), Indian yellow, alizarin crimson, phthalo blue, titanium white, midnight black, Van Dyke brown, Prussian blue, dark sienna, bright red, yellow ochre, cadmium yellow, sap green (+ a dark green, sap + Prussian (?)), Liquid White, Liquid Black.
- canvas: ~18x24 landscape (?). One thin even coat of Liquid Clear over the whole canvas [00:06], a tiny amount. No Liquid White, nothing pre-painted. Clear makes the surface wet and glossy, so the transparent sky colors blend easily and the black later stays black (adding white would dilute the color [12:39]).
- light: low sun at or just below the horizon, right of center (yellow-green glow x~0.55-1.0, y~0.35-0.42). Lit faces of mountain, foliage and rocks are warm (red, orange, ochre) on the sun side (right); shaded faces are Prussian blue (left side). The water mirrors the sky glow.

## Steps
### 00:30 Sky: yellow glow
- tool: 2-inch brush, bristles flat to the canvas.
- load: Indian yellow, the very smallest bit, tapped in.
- motion: tap color in, then drag horizontally left-right, light pressure, short runs; a few passes widen it to a soft band ~1/12 canvas height.
- placement: y~0.33-0.40 (upper-middle, below the top third), full width, heavier toward the right (?).
- result: a soft fuzzy yellow bar on the gray glossy clear.
- seen: 00:44, 00:52, 01:06

### 01:15 Sky: red/orange layer
- tool: same 2in, not washed.
- load: alizarin crimson on a brush with leftover yellow, so the first strokes are orange; later a bit more crimson with no yellow so it goes red.
- motion: horizontal side-to-side strokes directly above the yellow, then dragged down into it (blend it downward [01:44]); ~3 rounds with more crimson; spread it upward into the sky [02:58]. Tiny amounts.
- placement: red y~0.22-0.30, orange where it meets yellow (y~0.32), crimson fades upward by y~0.18.
- result: transparent layers, yellow base, orange middle, red top (04:52).
- rule: transparent colors laid over the clear need only a tiny amount of paint [03:04]
- seen: 01:40, 02:12, 02:44, 03:16

### 02:05 Water: mirror colors
- tool: same 2in.
- motion: same horizontal strokes in the lower half, repeated after each sky addition.
- placement: yellow y~0.60-0.66, red/pink y~0.68-0.78, centered x~0.25-0.8 as an ellipse under the sky glow. A pale gray-clear gap stays between sky glow and water (y~0.42-0.52).
- rule: whatever goes into the sky also goes a little into the water [02:13]; if the sky is brighter, the water should be brighter too [03:15]
- seen: 02:12, 02:44, 04:24

### 03:18 Blue
- tool: same unwashed 2in.
- load: phthalo blue + very little crimson, slight lavender but still strongly blue [03:38].
- motion: horizontal strokes from the top edge, brought down to y~0.18 and left to fade. In the water: along the water's top edge (y~0.50-0.58) and across the bottom (y~0.85-1.0); land can be painted right over this [04:10].
- result: blue-teal top, lavender where it overlaps crimson; blue frame around the glow.
- seen: 03:48, 04:20

### 04:26 Blend (dry brush)
- tool: second clean 2in, as dry as possible (wiped on paper towel). Clear + thinner react strongly [04:37].
- motion: light horizontal sweeps back and forth, top to bottom through sky then water, nearly no pressure; fades each color into the next; the gray gap stays pale.
- result: smooth horizontal gradient bands (04:35-04:52).
- seen: 04:35, 04:40, 04:46, 04:52

### 05:00 Streaky clouds
- tool: palette knife, flat; titanium white pulled out very flat on the palette, a thin roll cut off on the blade edge.
- motion: lay the blade edge on and rub horizontally, short rubs ~2-4 in, light pressure; simply rub it across the canvas [05:19]. Afterwards very lightly blend with a brush [05:38].
- placement: thin streaks along the top of the yellow band y~0.36-0.42, right half x 0.45-1.0; a couple in the red band y~0.28.
- result: crisp opaque white streaks against the transparent colors; after blending, softer (06:14).
- seen: 05:30, 06:14

### 05:50 Floating cloud
- tool: large brush; white; place it very quickly [06:01].
- placement: upper sky, x~0.5-0.75, y~0.12-0.2 (?); visible as a pale gray-pink wisp at 07:50.
- seen: 07:50

### 06:17 Big mountain
- tool: palette knife, long edge.
- load: black + Van Dyke + a little Prussian, mainly black; pulled out flat, roll of paint on the edge.
- motion: lay the roll along the outline, peak first, then let slopes drop off both sides; press hard, right into the canvas [07:09]; only the outside edge is the concern [06:54]. Then scrape off the excess with the knife, working it in thoroughly [07:19].
- placement: main peak x~0.43, y~0.15; second smaller peak x~0.54, y~0.27; long ridge falls to the right to x~0.78, y~0.40; left shoulder runs off the left edge at y~0.5. Base is soft at y~0.5-0.55.
- result: black silhouette against the orange glow (07:30, 07:50).
- seen: 06:52, 07:11, 07:30, 07:50

### 07:27 Haze at the base
- tool: large brush.
- motion: pull the wet black straight down in vertical strokes, then tap; this removes excess paint and suggests mist [07:40].
- result: base fades into gray-green haze y~0.5-0.6 over the pale gap.
- seen: 08:00, 08:50

### 08:09 Warm highlights
- tool: palette knife edge.
- load: dark sienna, bright red, titanium white, yellow ochre laid on the palette in layers, not over-mixed; blade cut straight across so all colors sit in one roll.
- motion: no pressure, strokes down the slope following the slope angle (about 35-50° down to the right), short ~2-3 in, many, black shows between. Apply some higher up on the big one and let it run down [09:04].
- placement: right (sun) faces of every peak, strongest just below the main ridgeline; interior left black.
- result: rust, red, tan, creamy streaks; mountain reads warm orange-brown on the right (09:14, 10:02).
- rule: the effect comes from how the knife is loaded, not from mixing [09:19]
- seen: 08:40, 09:14, 09:20

### 09:27 Blue shadows
- tool: small knife.
- load: Prussian blue + a little white, a touch of dark sienna, only lightly mixed [09:50].
- motion: pull down along the slope on left faces, edge, light; small projections where lit and shaded sides meet [10:18]; shift the colors between each other. A little light also shining through the dark area [11:00].
- placement: left plane of the main peak (x~0.38-0.43, y 0.18-0.45), the small left peak at x~0.28 (blue cap, y~0.30), crevices in the right peak.
- result: bright blue plane left of the peak, warm brown right: strong cool/warm split (10:50, 11:50).
- seen: 10:02, 10:26, 10:50, 11:50

### 11:27 Mist (tap)
- tool: clean dry 2in.
- motion: tap along the mountain base following the slope angles, then gently lift upward; repeated.
- result: pale veil at y~0.5-0.58 hiding the foot of the mountain.
- rule: angles must always be followed; this matters most [11:46]
- seen: 11:45, 12:00

### 12:00 Dark right mountain
- tool: knife edge, then brush.
- load: same black/Prussian/brown, it should appear black; roll on the edge.
- motion: from the left shoulder to the right in an arc, a bump (small peak) at the left [12:26], peak at the right; pressed on. Then take hold of that and pull it [12:54]: the brush pulls the wet paint down, soft base with vertical streaks (13:33).
- placement: peak x~0.84, y~0.37; slope falls left to x~0.2, y~0.52; base ~y 0.6; it sits in front of the first mountain, partially cutting its mist.
- result: near-black, no detail; hard edge against the yellow-green glow.
- rule: here we'll play with contrast [12:36]
- seen: 12:30, 13:00, 13:33, 13:50

### 13:14 Faint light on the dark mountain

### 16:42 Left foliage mass

## Rules and principles he states
- Clear: use very little; too much is the biggest mistake [01:50]. Works for transparent colors [00:16].
- Blend with a really dry brush on clear [04:31].
- Opaque white stands out against transparent colors [04:42].
- Mountain: only the outside edge matters [06:54]; scrape off excess [07:19].
- Black stays black on clear [12:39]. Contrast is the game here [12:36].
- Don't over-mix knife colors; the loading does the work [08:30, 09:19].
- Mist: tap, lift, follow the slope angle [11:46].
- Work back to front, finishing the most distant layer first [22:18].
- Sky color goes in the water, brighter where the sky is brighter [02:13, 03:15].
- Reuse the mountain colors on rocks and bark [21:14, 23:53].

## Finished painting
- focal point: main peak x~0.43, y~0.2 with its blue plane, against the glow.
- far: blue-lavender top sky, red-pink band, yellow-green glow at right (x 0.6-1.0, y~0.35) with white streak clouds; main mountain (brown lit right, blue left) with mist.
- middle: dark right mountain (peak x~0.84, y~0.37), green bank strip with yellow flecks, water line.
- near: water (yellow, pink, blue), dark banks with rocks at bottom left/right, yellow-green bushes; big left tree with orange foliage and white birch trunks; two thin bare trees on the right.
- darkest: right mountain and lower-left trees; lightest: yellow glow and white streaks (y~0.35-0.42), birch bark.
- proportions: sky ~0.45 (to the mountain base), mountains/bank ~0.2, water ~0.35.
- light from the right at horizon; palette warm center (orange/yellow/red) framed by cool blue-teal at the top, bottom and in the shadows.

## Surprises
- Clear coat, no white ground: all sky colors transparent, black stays black.
- Whole sky is horizontal bands from one unwashed brush (yellow, then crimson over it, then blue); no sun disc, only glow.
- Sky is mirrored in the water before any land is painted; land is laid over it.
- Mountain is first pure black; its color comes only from knife roll layers (sienna, red, white, ochre) plus a Prussian blue shadow side.
