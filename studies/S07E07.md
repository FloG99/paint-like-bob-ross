# S07E07 Barn at Sunset
- palette: titanium white, phthalo blue, midnight black, dark sienna, Van Dyke brown, alizarin crimson, cadmium yellow, yellow ochre, Indian yellow, bright red; liquid white (base coat, snow, thin trunks), liquid black (limbs).
- canvas: thin even coat of Liquid White, wet and slick. Nothing pre-painted. Whole canvas is later seen at 27:04 (credits overlay it, bottom/left reading is clean).
- light: winter dusk. Low sun glow sits behind the trees at the middle, about x 0.30-0.65, y 0.33-0.45. Yellow rim light on the tree edges facing the glow, pink-lavender cloud tops, cold blue-gray at the sky top and in the right trees. Warm bushes and warm wood are added on purpose so the snow scene does not read as cold. Direction of light is from behind the scene, centre (?).
- positions: fractions of the canvas, x from the left, y from the top, read from frames (approximate, ~0.03).

## Steps
### 00:30 Sky: three warm bands at the horizon
- tool: 2-in brush, flat face, same brush for all three colours (never cleaned between).
- load: tiny amount tapped into the bristles each time: Indian yellow first, then yellow ochre, then bright red.
- motion: straight horizontal strokes, one per colour, left to right, light pressure; each new band is laid directly against the previous one, edges touching. Then a very light horizontal pass where the colours meet.
- placement: whole width, x ~0.03-0.95; yellow bottom edge at y ~0.50 (this is the horizon), ochre above it, red top edge at y ~0.30. Ends are ragged, a bit short of both sides.
- result: yellow, orange, red ramp, white canvas still fully clean above and below (01:30, 01:38).
- rule: decide the horizon first [00:39]
- seen: 00:57, 01:11, 01:26, 01:40

### 01:40 Sky: black on top
- tool: 2-in brush, beaten out first; loaded with midnight black, thin.
- motion: small criss-cross strokes, working into the sky and letting the black thin out downward.
- placement: top left and centre, y 0-0.26, x ~0.05-0.75; white canvas stays between the black and the red band.
- result: blue-gray smudge at the top (02:13); it dilutes over the white to a steel blue (02:56).
- rule: contrast is the aim for this sky; it makes the warm band glow [01:51]
- seen: 01:56, 02:05, 02:13, 02:22

### 02:27 Sky: sienna cloud bodies
- tool: same brush, pulled through dark sienna on the palette.
- motion: turned in small circles, many tiny circles, dropped down from the blue into the colour band.
- placement: starts upper right (02:40, x ~0.5-0.9, y 0.05-0.30), then across the whole sky just above the red band (y ~0.20-0.35, 02:56-03:07).
- result: brown, ragged cloud mass between blue and red; blended lightly after so the colours step smoothly (03:25).
- seen: 02:40, 02:50, 02:58, 03:07, 03:16, 03:25

### 03:25 Sky: pink cloud lights
- tool: 1-in brush loaded by pulling through white with a little bright red (warm pink); blue added later for cool parts.
- motion: jiggling, small curls, brush never stops; paint dropped in the places where blue meets brown. Many clouds, any size.
- placement: first cluster left (x 0.05-0.30), then upper right and centre (x 0.45-0.95), tall mass right at x 0.65-0.9, y 0.05-0.40. Some tops are bluer, the lit tops pinker.
- result: bumpy cumulus with brown underside and blue holes (03:51, 04:10, 04:32).
- rule: do not kill the clear blue areas by over-blending [04:28]
- seen: 03:42, 03:51, 04:00, 04:10, 04:21, 04:32, 04:43

### 04:19 Cloud soften and fluff
- tool: clean dry 2-in brush.
- motion: gentle blend of the underside, then tiny circles to fluff the tops [06:03]; light touch.
- result: soft bottoms melting into the red band, crisp bumpy tops (05:17, 05:28, 06:15).
- seen: 04:55, 05:06, 05:17, 05:28, 05:40, 05:50, 06:02, 06:15

### 06:23 Sun streaks
- tool: palette knife, cut edge.
- load: straight cadmium yellow, a tiny roll on the edge.
- motion: knife laid almost flat, barely grazing the canvas, short horizontal drags letting the paint skip.
- placement: across the red/orange band, x 0.0-0.3 at y ~0.43, also a few thinner streaks at y ~0.38 and y ~0.47 on the right (07:05, 07:17).
- result: thin pale-yellow dashes in the band.
- then: clean dry brush stroked very lightly over them, then a little liquid white pulled down under the yellow so the horizon dissolves into pale mist, y ~0.50-0.65 (07:17).
- seen: 06:27, 06:40, 06:52, 07:05, 07:17, 07:30

### 07:43 Background trees
- tool: round brush (?), tapped.
- load: black + phthalo blue + a little alizarin crimson, mixed on the palette; a touch more later makes lavender.
- motion: taps only, small basic tree masses, ragged top edges, built from the bottom upward so the tops come out lighter. Done before any barn.
- placement: first cluster at x ~0.25-0.45, low in the glow (08:28); then a line left to right (08:45, 09:17); tall dark mass on the right x 0.65-0.95 reaching y ~0.25-0.30; tree line base ~y 0.65-0.70 (?).
- result: dark violet masses against the orange (09:33, 09:50); one cloud was partly covered at 09:17.
- rule: leave the pretty sky areas uncovered; plan gaps [08:32]
- seen: 08:12, 08:28, 08:45, 09:01, 09:17, 09:33, 09:50

### 09:40 Trunks
- tool: liner brush with thinner, Van Dyke, twisted to a point.
- motion: thin crooked lines with a wiggle, a few trunks and branches; not straight.
- placement: one clear bare trunk in the large right-hand tree at x ~0.73, y 0.35-0.55; others small between the trees.
- seen: 10:23, 10:47, 11:35

### 10:55 Warm and cold highlights on the trees
- tool: round brush.
- load: yellow + alizarin crimson + white + bright red for the warm; blue + white and red + white (lavender) for the cool.
- motion: very light taps on tops and on edges toward the glow; little, colour is added later. One white-lavender tuft tapped on a dark tree stands out.
- placement: warm taps at the left side of trees near the glow (x 0.3-0.6), cool taps on the upper right tree tops.
- result: yellow-green rim along the dark masses (10:47, 11:35), pink-lavender foliage tops (11:58), a lavender-white snowy bush at about (0.50, 0.65) (12:46).
- seen: 11:11, 11:35, 11:58, 12:22, 12:46

### 13:17 Snow ground
- tool: 2-in brush, then fan brush.
- load: leftover pink, white, blue mix and shadow colour picked from the palette; no new tube.
- motion: laid across the open lower canvas in the direction the land runs (slopes down toward the right (?)); grab paint from the bottom of the trees and pull it down; fan brush lifts upward at the tree base for a soft misty edge.
- placement: from y ~0.62 to the bottom, whole width.
- result: pale lavender-white snow field, soft mist at the tree base (13:45, 14:22).
- seen: 13:32, 13:45, 13:57, 14:10, 14:22, 14:35

### 14:55 Barn block-in with the knife
- tool: palette knife, tip, then flat blade.
- motion: thin scraped lines show the shape, not committed: roof ridge, the two roof edges, the wall corner, a lower wing out to each side (long lines from the gable out sideways). Then the blade is dragged flat to remove the paint from the whole barn area.
- placement: scraped lines run through the tree line and snow: ridge peak at about (0.61, 0.43); right rake goes down-right to (0.80, 0.56); left roof edge runs down-left toward (0.33, 0.58); wing lines continue sideways at y ~0.57-0.70 (read from the finished canvas and 15:36).
- result: bare, scraped zone with fine pale lines on the tree layer (15:36, 16:07).
- rule: scraping out the shape shows the perspective, and removing the wet paint makes the next layer stick better [14:57]
- seen: 15:00, 15:11, 15:22, 15:33, 15:45, 15:56, 16:07

### 16:12 Overhang and gable wall
- tool: 2-in brush, loaded full with Van Dyke; twisted/wiggled and pulled to a sharp chisel edge. Both sides of the brush carry paint.
- motion: first the roof edge: one dark diagonal line along the right rake (from upper left down to the right, about 25-30 degrees below horizontal), then the wall: long strokes pulled straight down from the rake to the base, the brush turned to use both edges. Strokes ragged at the bottom.
- placement: dark rake line from (0.55, 0.43) to (0.82, 0.57); wall below it from the rake down to y ~0.82; left edge ~x 0.43, right edge ~x 0.80.
- result: a black-brown block with vertical streaks, lower edge ragged (16:47, 16:55).
- seen: 16:40, 16:47, 16:55

### 17:10 Side wings
- tool: same brush.
- motion: extra block pulled down at each side of the gable: a low wing to the right of the wall (flat top slightly lower, y ~0.57 right) and the left shed part at the lower left; keep it loose, still changeable.
- placement: right wing x 0.76-0.90, y 0.56-0.80; left wing starts at x ~0.28, top y ~0.60.
- result: the whole building as a dark silhouette, gable plus two low wings (17:17, 17:32).
- seen: 17:02, 17:10, 17:17, 17:25, 17:32

### 17:30 Old wood
- tool: same brush, wiped; loaded with white and dark sienna.
- motion: drag over the dark paint with no pressure at all, brush barely touching, tiny skips; hand tremor helps, it makes grain. Repeat on the right wing, then a light touch on the left wing. Downward, along the board direction.
- placement: whole gable wall first, then right wing wall, then a bit of the left part.
- result: dark wall becomes broken tan-brown streaky wood with a cross-hatched dry texture (17:58, 18:11, 18:24).
- rule: nearly no pressure gives the weathered grain [17:44]
- seen: 17:45, 17:58, 18:11, 18:24

### 18:34 Shadows and boards
- tool: 2-in brush with Van Dyke, then palette knife with Van Dyke.
- motion: brush: barely touching, places the shadow tone at the wall bases and under the wing. Knife: loaded with a cut-off roll; edge laid vertical and pulled down to draw dark seams between boards, spacing uneven, some boards wide, a few short. Then clean brush pulls down gently to merge.
- placement: seams on the gable wall mostly in its right half, and on both wings; a couple of long slightly leaning cracks.
- result: gray-brown boards with dark seams (19:03, 19:16, 19:30).
- rule: loose, uneven boards read as old wood [19:09]
- seen: 18:37, 18:50, 19:03, 19:16, 19:30

### 19:36 Door

- load: dark brown / black.
- motion: a rectangle pulled down; the corners at the top are cut on a slant, then rounded with the small knife blade pulled down; later a thin light-brown line is drawn along the outline.
- placement: below the peak, slightly right: x ~0.58-0.71, y ~0.74-0.84 on the finished canvas; stands on the snow line. The door width is about a third of the gable wall.
- result: dark opening with chamfered top (20:03-21:30), light edge (22:18, 24:00).
- rule: the light edge makes the door stand out [21:12]
- seen: 19:49, 20:18, 20:32, 21:15, 21:30, 22:18

### 19:56 Snow roofs
- tool: palette knife, laid with the edge and then smoothed with the flat blade.
- load: white with a trace of pink; liquid white for the thin line.
- motion: laid along the roof planes in strokes that follow the slope, one stroke per row of snow, snow ends in a straight line at the eave; thin dark under-edge left bare at the rake. Pulled down at the lower edge for a soft thickness.
- placement: left roof slope from the ridge (0.61, 0.43) down-left to about (0.33, 0.58); lower lean-to at the left, x ~0.22-0.46, y ~0.64-0.72; a thin line of snow on top of the right wing at y ~0.57. The right rake keeps a dark edge with a thin pink-white line on it.
- result: smooth pale roof with faint row lines, brown wall below (20:46, 21:15, 22:03).
- rule: roof is blocked in with a few big knife strokes; it also serves as a guide for perspective [20:09]
- seen: 20:03, 20:18, 20:32, 20:46, 21:01, 22:03, 22:46

- placement: tall bare tree at the left, x ~0.05-0.20, y 0.0-0.90, fork at about y 0.12; smaller fork at x 0.17-0.24.
- result: dark branching trunk against the pink sky, light trunk edge (26:28, 27:04).
- seen: 25:15, 25:56, 26:28

## Rules and principles he states
- [00:39] choose the horizon first.
- [04:28] blend lightly, over-blending kills the sky.
- [08:32] do not cover the pretty sky areas when adding trees.
- [08:52] start low and work up so the top gets lighter and the shadows stay at the bottom.
- [14:57] scrape the barn shape with the knife for perspective; scraping also removes wet paint so the next layer sticks.
- [17:44] no pressure for old wood.
- [23:23] warm colours in bushes keep the snow from being cold.

## Finished painting
- focal point: the barn, peak at (0.61, 0.43), pale snowy roof against the orange glow; door at (0.65, 0.79).
- planes: far = sky, clouds, glow; middle = tree line and big right trees, the lavender bush left; near = barn, snow slope, bushes, blue strip at the bottom, tall bare tree at the left.
- darkest darks: door opening, wall under the eave, the Van Dyke tree trunk at the left, shadow side of the dark right trees; lightest lights: snow roof, cloud tops (x 0.45-0.65, y 0.05-0.15) and snow slope at lower left.
- framing: tall bare tree at left, big blue-gray tree mass at right.
- proportions: sky ~30-35%, tree line ~20%, barn and land ~35%, blue strip ~10% (?).
- light direction: glow behind the middle, rim light on tree edges, barn lit on the front by the low sun (?).
- temperature: warm orange/pink centre against blue-gray top and the cold blue strip; barn wood neutral brown.

## Surprises
- The sky has three horizontal colour bands laid before anything else, and black goes on top afterward.
- The trees go in first, then the barn area is scraped and wiped out with the knife; the barn is painted over the cleared area.
- The barn begins as a dark Van Dyke block with two chisel-edge brush strokes; the weathered wood comes from light, nearly pressureless strokes of white and sienna over it.
- Snow on the roofs is laid with a knife, row by row.
- The shape is thought of first as lines scraped through the paint, not as a drawn plan.
- Blue is introduced late as a single saturated strip at the bottom.
