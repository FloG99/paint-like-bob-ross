# S12E13 Winter Mountain
- palette: alizarin crimson (on-screen caption reads bright red at 0:47; he says alizarin), phthalo blue, midnight black, Prussian blue, Van Dyke brown, titanium white, liquid white. No green, no yellow (green avoided on purpose, 21:32).
- canvas: Liquid White base coat, wet and slick (0:19). Nothing pre-painted.
- light: from the right / behind the mountain: pink glow in the sky right of center; lit faces of the peaks are the right-hand (sky-facing) slopes, left faces stay blue-gray shadow (? read from frames 7:10-9:30 and the finished frame). Cold, still, dusk-like winter; one small warm crimson spot in the sky is his choice (0:49).

## Steps
### 0:23 Sky: pink glow
- tool: 2-inch brush, bristle face.
- load: alizarin crimson, small amount, tapped firmly on the palette so it goes evenly through the bristles; a little more at 1:11.
- motion: short scrubbing strokes (?) building a horizontal patch; light pressure.
- placement: one blob right of center, about y 0.2-0.4, x 0.35-0.85 (frames 0:47, 1:14, 1:41).
- result: soft pink cloud, wide and horizontal.
- rule: he does not want the sky set on fire [1:15], only a pinkish glow.
- seen: 0:47, 1:14

### 1:28 Sky: blue
- tool: same 2-in brush, not cleaned.
- load: small phthalo blue tapped in; blue is much stronger than crimson so no cleaning [1:31].
- motion: little criss-cross X strokes, worked in with a light bouncing motion, light pressure; start at the top, work down to the pink and into it; the blue kills the crimson at the contact edge.
- placement: whole top of the canvas down to the pink, filling both top corners (frame 2:08).
- result: blue top, pink band in the middle, pale below.
- seen: 1:41, 2:08

### 2:08 Lower blue
- tool: same brush.
- load: phthalo blue, a touch of crimson dropped in here and there at 2:37.
- motion: dropped in with horizontal strokes (?), quick; also unloads the brush.
- placement: lower third, full width; pale light blue with some lavender (frames 2:35, 3:02).
- rule: this will end up as shadows in the snow or the water [2:13].
- seen: 2:35, 3:02

### 2:47 Sky blend
- tool: 2-in brush washed (scrub, shake off, splatter), clean and dry.
- load: none.
- motion: very light criss-cross X strokes; start in the light area and work toward the dark; continue until no line shows between colors.
- placement: pink/blue boundary at top, and the pink/blue boundary below.
- result: smooth gradient; the pink band still reads in the middle (frame 3:30).
- rule: begin the blend in the lighter zone and move toward the darker one [3:20].
- seen: 3:02, 3:30

### 3:40 Mountain: dark silhouette
- tool: painting knife, flat of the blade; paint is a roll on the edge.
- load: midnight black + Prussian blue + Van Dyke + a bit of crimson (aim for near-black). Pull the paint out flat as possible, then cut across it so a roll of paint sits on the blade edge [4:01].
- motion: the roll is dragged along the canvas, lifted and re-placed to make a jagged ridge line; peaks are built by up/down zigzag of the roll. Light pressure.
- placement: main peak summit around x 0.25-0.3, y 0.1-0.2 (?); ridge drops right with a second peak, wider mass to the left; spans most of the canvas width at y 0.2-0.35 (frames 4:17-5:12). Sits over the pink band.
- result: dark, blotchy jagged silhouette with canvas-weave texture.
- seen: 4:17, 4:35, 4:54, 5:12

### 4:58 Mountain: scrape and blend
- tool: knife blade held flat, then a brush (the 2-in, washed at 5:55) (?).
- motion: scrape off all the excess with the blade, firm (get tough) [4:58]; then pull firmly downward from the ridge so the remaining paint smears into the wet canvas as a gray haze; blend with the brush in short light strokes; soften points and peaks.
- placement: haze runs from the ridge down through the pink into the lower blue, to about y 0.55-0.6.
- result: dark ridge with smoky fade downward; no hard bottom edge (frames 5:31, 5:50).
- rule: the less excess paint left behind, the simpler the next step [5:12].
- seen: 4:54, 5:31, 5:50, 6:15

### 6:14 Mountain: lit side, white
- tool: knife, edge roll of white, same loading (flat, cut across).
- load: straight titanium white, no color.
- motion: touch the roll to the canvas at a peak and let it flow straight down the slope with no pressure; only the roll touches the canvas; short pulls that leave dry streaky texture following the slope; reload when out. All strokes keep basically the same angle.
- placement: only the right-facing slope of each peak (falling to the right, roughly 45-60° (?)), starting under the ridge and trailing down toward the haze; the left faces stay dark.
- result: streaky, broken white over dark with the dark showing through (frames 7:10, 7:38, 8:06).
- rule: keep every angle roughly identical, because light hits from one direction [7:21]. Apply no force [6:36].
- seen: 7:10, 7:38, 8:06

### 8:20 Mountain: shadow side
- tool: knife.
- load: titanium white + Prussian blue (little, since it is very strong) giving a light blue-white; fresh roll cut.
- motion: where one peak sits behind another, do the back one first with a small pull, then the front peak flows over it, which sends the rear one farther away [9:04]. For each highlight drop a blue shadow beside it on the other side of the ridge; firm up the edge with a small pull; bring strokes together so they flow.
- placement: left faces and hollows between the peaks, and in the crease below the main summit (frames 9:02, 9:30, 10:04).
- result: lit faces read white, shadow faces blue-white; peaks separate.
- rule: each light stroke needs a dark counterpart beside it [9:25]; a peak given its own shadow jumps forward clearly [10:45].
- seen: 8:34, 9:02, 9:30, 9:40

### 9:40 More peaks and lower snowfield
- tool: knife, white then blue-white.
- motion: small peaks added anywhere: highlight stroke first, shadow dropped beside it. Shadows are curved patches following slope contours; lower slopes get broad light white swipes that sweep outward and down in arcs (frames 10:28-11:17).
- placement: extra small peak at left edge (summit x ~0.1, y ~0.22); white snowfield swings left from the main summit down to y ~0.45; curved short blue shadow ribs in the lower left of the main face.
- result: big central peak with white lower flanks and blue crevice patches.
- seen: 10:04, 10:28, 10:52, 11:17

### 11:51 Mountain base mist

### 17:23 Evergreens
- tool: 1-inch brush; corner of the brush for the tree.
- load: dark mix (midnight black + Prussian blue + Van Dyke, mixed at 17:15), no green: green pulled into snow looks wrong [21:34]. Lots of paint, or the bristles will not hold together [17:48].
- motion: pull the brush through the paint and wiggle while pulling, then sharpen to a chisel edge [17:35]. Tap downward with one corner at the apex (a thin line), work back and forth left/right, widening each tier, with more pressure each time so a bigger part of the brush touches; keep to the same corner all the way down. Reload after each tree [19:13]. Result is tiers of short horizontal taps with ragged tips.
- placement: first thin tree at x ~0.5, apex y ~0.15 (frame 18:08); a bigger one in front (18:32); small ones left (18:57); a very big tree right of center at x ~0.6-0.8 running off the top of the canvas, overlapping the mountain (19:45, 20:10); more at the far right (21:03). Bottoms end ragged at roughly the lake edge / snow line.
- result: layered dark blue spruces with sky holes; the big trees cover part of the mountain, which he accepts [19:42].
- rule: press harder with each step down so a larger part of the brush meets the canvas [18:13].
- seen: 17:44, 18:08, 18:32, 18:57, 19:45, 20:10, 20:44, 21:03, 21:22, 21:42, 22:01

## Rules and principles he states
- A tiny crimson spot warms a winter sky; omit it for colder [0:49].
- Blue overpowers crimson, so no cleaning between [1:31].
- Blend the sky starting in the light area, toward the dark [3:20].
- Mountain: flatten paint, cut a roll on the knife edge [4:01]; scrape off all excess [4:58]; the roll alone touches canvas, no pressure [6:36, 8:00].
- Keep highlight angles the same, light comes at one angle [7:21].
- For every highlight a shadow [9:25]; a peak looks weak until it has its own shadow [10:38].
- Back peak first, front peak flows over it [8:48].
- Distant trees are basic shapes, details come in the foreground [13:39].
- Pull down for instant reflections [14:53].
- Evergreens need a lot of paint on the brush [17:48]; one corner all the way down, more pressure as you descend [18:13].
- No green in winter evergreens (green snow looks wrong) [21:34].
- Snow shadows: pull dark under trees, then lay white over [23:13].
- Snow bushes need dark behind them [25:13].
- Keep contrast in the last highlights [26:41].

## Finished painting
- focal point: main snowy peak, summit at about x 0.27, y 0.08; lit snowfield below it (x 0.15-0.45, y 0.2-0.5).
- planes: far = peaks and pink/blue sky (y 0-0.5); middle = island of small blue-gray spruces and pale lake (y 0.5-0.75); near = snow banks with path and bushes (y 0.75-1.0), plus big dark spruces on the right (x 0.55-0.95), a tall thin spruce at x ~0.52, and smaller spruces at left (x 0-0.3).
- darkest darks: right-hand big spruces and left-edge trees; lightest lights: lit mountain slopes, snow bushes, snow crest.
- framing: spruce clusters left and right with an open center for lake and peak.
- proportions: sky ~20%, mountain ~30%, lake ~20% (pale, glass-like, white horizontal lines), snow ~25%.
- light: from the right/behind, pink glow right of the peak; shadows and snow are blue; palette cold overall, pink glow is the only warm.

## Surprises
- Mountain is built dark first (knife silhouette, scraped, pulled down), then lit with white knife rolls on right-facing slopes only, shadows dropped in afterward.
- Evergreens use the 1-inch brush corner, with no green on purpose.
- The lake is open water with liquid-white knife lines; the only frozen-looking element is the pale-blue path/stream in the snow, which he calls a path.
- Snow-bank shadows come from dragging dark tree mix into white with the same 2-in brush, no separate shadow step.
