# S17E07 Spectacular Waterfall
- palette: phthalo blue, titanium white, cadmium yellow, alizarin crimson, bright red, Van Dyke brown, dark sienna, yellow ochre, Indian yellow, black, Prussian blue, phthalo green, sap green; liquid clear (falls, pool, under-layer), liquid white (sky). Lavender = alizarin crimson + touch phthalo blue (+ white as needed).
- canvas: PORTRAIT, 18 by 24 pre-stretched (?) (frames at 27:04 show a tall canvas; the finished.webp is a landscape crop of its upper ~55%). Pre-done off-camera: black gesso over everything except the sky window, with rough dark trees/cliffs/ground indicated; thin coat of liquid clear over the black; lavender over all the black; liquid white over the sky window. The gesso supplies the dark trees and dark masses, which are only touched up later.
- light: from the left (stated 04:37); cool twilight/dusk sky, pale yellow-green glow under the mountain; whole painting violet-blue; falls lavender-white.
- coordinates below: x 0 = left edge, 1 = right edge of canvas; y 0 = top, 1 = bottom of the tall canvas (read off the 27:04 frame, +-0.04).

## Rules and principles he states
- [01:16] Thin transparent blue lets the pre-painted shapes show: do not trace them.
- [02:14] Lavender flowing into the sky edge gives a soft, misty effect at a distance.
- [03:39] Protect the pale glow at the foot of the mountain.
- [05:07] Mountain highlights with no pressure; let the knife float and disappear.
- [06:20] Tap along the angles and lift upward: soft and distant.
- [11:24] Pull rock blends in the direction of the rock form, never across.
- [12:00] Liquid clear on the brush thins white so it flows smooth.
- [13:30] Falls: colour where the light strikes (top); softer and darker lower.
- [14:27] Do not over-mix: marbled paint gives many effects.
- [15:21] Keep rocks dark; highlights few.
- [19:45] Splashes: push upward and bend the bristles.
- [21:00] Few branches so the mountain shows through the tree.
- [22:16] Do not kill the dark area; it frames the light.

## Finished painting
- finished.webp is a 4:3 landscape crop of the upper part (no pool, bushes or foreground bank); on the tall canvas the full composition runs sky/mountain (y 0.03-0.40), falls (y 0.46-0.72), pool (0.72-0.85), bank (0.85-0.97).
- focal point: the lit mountain with its glow (x ~0.45, y 0.17-0.40) and the bright falls lip (x ~0.40, y 0.46) form a vertical pair; the falls are the largest bright mass (x 0.18-0.60, y 0.46-0.70).
- planes: far = mountain + glow + distant pines (y 0.17-0.42); middle = falls, cliffs both sides, tall pine; near = pool, stone, bushes, foreground bank.
- darkest darks: dark tree masses at the left and right edges, the cliffs flanking the falls, the ground below the pines; lightest lights: top of the falls, mist at the base, mountain highlight faces, horizon glow.
- framing: dark tree and cliff mass left, tall pine and cliff right, forming a window; falls sit left of centre below the mountain; tall pine right of the mountain.
- proportions (tall canvas): sky ~10%, mountain ~20%, falls + cliffs ~30%, pool and bank ~30%.
- light from the left; palette cool violet and blue, with warm tan/sienna in the rocks and bank, yellow-green in the glow and bushes.

## Surprises
- Canvas is portrait and the falls sit in the middle, not at the bottom; the finished.webp crop is misleading.
- Black gesso pre-painted the dark trees and masses before the show; trees are only touched up.
- The whole falls is one wide liquid-clear + white sheet (1in brush), started next to the rock and added to leftward, not a thin stream.
- Mist at the base is painted AFTER the falls, by tapping then softening; the earlier mist was a dab pattern tapped on at 08:38.
- Rocks are made with only a fan brush, a barely-touching knife and a clean brush pulled in the rock direction.
