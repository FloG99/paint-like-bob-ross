# S01E06 Winter Moon
- palette: titanium white, Van Dyke brown, Prussian blue (the pair, mixed, is the only dark used), liquid white (called magic white here), thin oil/clear for the signature. Other tubes visible on the palette (yellow, red) are not used.
- canvas: black canvas, already coated before the episode with a thin transparent layer of Van Dyke brown + Prussian blue; it still reads black [0:07-0:12]. The black is meant to show through (any transparent color works on it) [16:50-17:01]. Nothing else pre-painted.
- light: a full moon, slightly right of canvas center and about a quarter down (see Finished painting for the fractions); night, extreme cold, still. Snow and ice-frosted bushes are the lit parts, everything else is left near-black so it reads as night.
- framing note: the camera alternates between a wide shot (canvas seen steeply from the side, foreshortened) and close-ups whose crop and zoom change from shot to shot. Fractions below are read from the close-up closest in time and marked (?) when the crop is uncertain. Frame quality is low (soft, interlaced smear on hand movement), so edge detail and exact brush identity are partly guessed.

## Steps
### 0:26 Moon glow: white worked outward
- tool: a short bristle brush, looks about 1-2 in (?); held with the bristle face to the canvas.
- load: titanium white, straight.
- motion: small X strokes, short and crossing, starting at the center of the future moon and spiraling outward in a ring, light even pressure. White is thin enough that it picks up the blue-brown underneath and turns gray-blue at the rim [0:55]. After the round halo he pulls strokes out to the right and slightly down toward the horizon, so the glow bounces back up off the curve of the earth [0:47-0:53] (frame 0:50 shows a roughly round fluffy halo with a streak trailing right and low).
- placement: halo centered near x 0.5, y 0.25 (?), about 0.25-0.3 of the canvas width across; a horizontal glow band at about y 0.6-0.7 (?) across most of the width; below that the canvas stays black (frames 1:02, 1:14, 1:38).
- result: brightest at the middle, darker toward every edge; the band at the horizon is soft and wide.
- rule: begin at the center and work outward so the light fades with distance [0:39-0:45]; keep it dim, it must still read as night [1:05-1:12].
- seen: 0:50, 1:02, 1:14, 1:38
### 1:21 Glow blend
- tool: large brush (2 in or more, ?), clean, dry-ish.
- motion: very gentle X strokes all over the white, a few passes across the full width [1:26-1:47].
- result: the white and the black merge into a smoky gray-blue haze; the very top edge and corners stay black. A scatter of hard streaks remains, which he treats as fine texture [1:39].
- seen: 1:26, 1:38, 1:50
### 1:47 The moon: pressed in, edge trimmed, then softened
- tool: a small round-ish brush (frame 2:04 shows the brush end-on, held pointing straight at the canvas, ferrule about the size of the disc), then the painting knife for trimming, then the large brush.
- load: brush loaded full of titanium white, a thick dab [1:49-1:53].
- motion: set straight into the canvas at the chosen spot, push hard, and give it one twist to press the paint into the surface [1:57-2:06]. This leaves a raised, ringed disc with a visible thick rim (frame 2:11). The knife blade is brought flat against the disc from below-left and slid to take off the excess paint around the rim [2:08-2:11]. The large brush is cleaned [2:15-2:24] and used very lightly, back and forth across the disc and its halo [2:25-2:32].
- placement: x ~0.5, y ~0.25-0.3 (?), at the center of the glow, no offset from the halo; disc about 0.04-0.06 of canvas width before blending (frames 2:11, 2:34).
- result: a small bright oval disc, edge softened but not gone, brighter than any glow, with a soft circular halo about five times its diameter (frame 2:34).
- rule: push the paint into the canvas rather than lay it on top [2:02]; the knife removes only the excess, not the disc.
- seen: 1:54, 2:04, 2:11, 2:17, 2:34

## Rules and principles he states
- Start the glow at the center and work outward, it should get darker with distance [0:39-0:45].
- Do not let the glow get bright, it has to read as night [1:05].
- Moon: full brush of white, push in hard, twist; knife takes off excess; large clean brush softens [1:49-2:32].
- Clouds go in dark first with a lot of paint pushed hard; lights come later [3:00-3:25].
- Light on a cloud sits on the side facing the moon: top for clouds below, bottom for clouds above [4:54-5:02].
- Unplanned cloud shapes are welcome [5:41-5:45].
- Remove all excess paint from a mountain with a brush pull [6:24-6:30].
- Mountain snow: very delicate touch, break and play [7:12-7:17]; every highlight needs its shadow [8:12].
- Mist: tap along slope angles, then lift up [8:52-9:16].
- Dark first, so that light will show [9:50]; thin paint sticks to thick [10:28].
- Pull straight down for reflections, then sweep across for a watery effect [11:32-11:50].
- Use size, color and more to show distance; trees larger toward the viewer [14:49, 15:17].
- Black canvas: any transparent color lets the black show through; use the black [16:58-17:01].
- Shadow color first, then highlight it [18:12]; leave dark areas, do not cover them all or it goes flat [21:39-21:43].
- Layers of bushes, one at a time, for distance [21:58-22:01].
- Normally trees are left very dark for a stronger night feel [27:25-27:28].

## Finished painting
- focal point: the pale moon, at about x 0.5, y 0.25-0.3 in the wide shot (?), appears at x 0.69, y 0.2 in the cropped final close-up; the long cloud streak through it and the white peak below give the area the highest contrast.
- value scheme: sky mid-dark gray-blue with the black underpaint at the top corners; the moon the lightest note of the sky, with a halo that fades to black. The snow on the peaks and the frosted bushes are brighter than the sky halo, creating the brightest values of the picture; the darkest darks are the pines, the lake and the lower border. Highest contrast is on the pine/snow-bush edges.
- planes: far = sky, clouds and two peaks (y 0-0.55); middle = row of small frosted bushes and tiny pines with the lake in front (y 0.55-0.8); near = big pale bushes at right, dark pines at far left and right edges, cabin and snowbank lower left (y 0.65-1.0).
- framing elements: dark pines on the left and right edges, with the lake and peaks open between.
- moonlight on snow: snow is blue-white (white + a touch of blue) with dark specks; the lit side of the peaks is the one facing the moon on the right (?).
- moonlight on water: the lake is dark with vertical blue-white streaks from the bush reflections, plus a thin white shore line; no reflection of the moon itself is painted.
- proportions: sky ~30%, mountains ~25%, lake and bushes ~30%, ground ~15% (?).
- temperature: cold throughout, deep blue-gray with white; the cabin roof reads slightly cream/yellowish in the final frame, probably the camera (?).

## Surprises
- The canvas starts black, and the moon and glow are made by adding white onto the dark ground; the sky is not painted dark, it is the dark itself.
- The moon is a pushed-in dab (twisted, trimmed with the knife, then softened), not a lifted or wiped-out hole.
- The glow is built before the moon and before the clouds; clouds are painted dark first, lit later, with the light side chosen by the moon's position.
- A band of glow also runs along the horizon, bounced up off the earth's curve, while the ground below is left black.
- Snow-covered bushes and frost are pushed in as thin paint on thick dark; reflections are single straight pulls with a clean brush picking up the dark ground.
- Trees are left dark on purpose; the blue highlights on them are only added for the viewer at home.
- The lake carries no mirrored moon; its only light comes from the bush reflections and a white line.
