# S11E01 Mountain Stream
- palette: phthalo blue, midnight black, titanium white, bright red, Van Dyke brown, alizarin crimson, Prussian blue, sap green, dark sienna, cadmium yellow, yellow ochre, Indian yellow (touch). Liquid White on the canvas.
- canvas: Liquid White, thin and wet (01:02). Nothing pre-painted. Landscape format.
- light: from the right (10:21): snow and highlights on right faces, shadows on left faces. Daytime, cool, clear blue sky with cumulus, haze at the mountain base. Warm only in the yellow-green of the near banks.
- positions are fractions of the canvas, x from the left edge, y from the top. Frames are often zoomed, so positions are approximate (?).

## Steps

### 01:21 Sky: blue and black, X strokes
- tool: old 2 in brush, flat, hair against the canvas.
- load: tapped into phthalo blue, then a bit of midnight black; mixed on the brush only (? about 1:1; reads dark navy). Thick load.
- motion: little X / criss-cross strokes, short (~3-4 in), brush near flat to the canvas, firm pressure; the brush jumps about. Add paint at 02:03 and continue.
- placement: top ~45% of the canvas. Two blue masses, upper left (x 0-0.4) and upper right (x 0.55-1.0), with a pale V-shaped gap between them at x ~0.4-0.6 running down from the top, plus small open spots. Darkest at the top corners.
- result: hard-edged blue patches on white with open spaces kept free for clouds.
- rule: leave small open patches in the sky [02:12]
- seen: 01:50, 02:00, 02:10, 02:20, 02:30

### 02:20 Sky: blend toward the horizon
- tool: same 2 in brush, not cleaned.
- motion: strokes pulled downward from the blue edge so the blue fades into the liquid white; mostly at the lower edge of the blue masses (y ~0.4-0.55).
- rule: blending downward draws the white into the color [02:25]
- seen: 02:30, 02:42

### 02:42 Water slab: back and forth
- tool: same dirty 2 in brush, reloaded with the blue + black pile (dark navy).
- motion: horizontal back-and-forth strokes, brush flat, full width; paint dropped in as needed. Also a way to clean excess paint off the brush [03:21].
- placement: lower ~45% of the canvas, y ~0.57 to the bottom, x 0.05-0.95, top edge nearly horizontal, slightly domed. This becomes the stream: the land is painted around it later.
- result: dark blue slab with horizontal streak marks.
- rule: it does not matter where the paint ends up [03:13]
- seen: 03:06, 03:18, 03:30

### 03:28 Clean and blend
- tool: clean dry 2 in brush. Cleaning: scrub on the screen in the thinner can, shake off, beat against the can, dry.
- motion: diagonal/criss-cross strokes in the sky from the top down, then long horizontal passes across the horizon (y ~0.55) to soften the sky/water junction; a few light horizontal passes over the slab.
- result: smooth sky, light at the horizon; pale band at y ~0.55 above the water slab, which is mid blue and lightens toward the horizon.
- rule: dry brush so the paint stays thick; a thin layer adheres to a thick one [04:09]
- seen: 03:50, 03:59, 04:08, 04:17, 04:26, 04:35

### 04:36 Clouds: drop in
- tool: 1 in brush, flat, then corner.
- load: pull the brush through titanium white (both sides), add the smallest trace of bright red (reads pale pink-white); load a lot of paint.
- motion: press on at the cloud position, push/scrub short curved strokes to build a lumpy rounded top; base left flat/ragged. Drop and leave.
- placement: about three masses in the open sky gaps (?): upper right-center (largest, x 0.55-0.9, y 0.05-0.3), upper left (x 0.05-0.3, y 0.1-0.3), smaller one upper center (x 0.4-0.5, y 0.05-0.2).
- result: white cumulus with flat undersides.
- rule: once placed, leave the clouds alone and do not overwork them [06:00]
- seen: 04:55, 05:11, 05:27, 05:43, 05:59

### 06:15 Clouds: base blend
- tool: clean dry brush (1 in or 2 in (?)), corner only.
- motion: tiny circles, barely touching, along the bottom of each cloud only, never the top. Few passes; stir a little.
- result: base dissolves into the blue, top stays crisp.
- rule: the more you fiddle, the more the cloud fades away [06:45]
- seen: 06:32, 06:49

### 07:04 Clouds: fluff the tops
- tool: same brush, beaten once against the can (no full wash).
- motion: a feather-light touch with minimal pressure, barely touching the top, small circular strokes lifting up and around; never straight up.
- rule: always use circular strokes [07:16]
- seen: 07:06, 07:23, 07:40

### 07:53 Mountain: dark shape with the knife
- tool: large palette knife (flat on the palette to mix, edge on the canvas).
- load: midnight black + Van Dyke brown + little alizarin crimson + Prussian blue; more crimson only for purple. Spread very flat on the palette, slice across so a small roll of paint sits on the blade edge.
- motion: drop in the jagged ridge line with short stabs and slides at the slope angles, building a stepped peak with a long thin tail running off to the right and a stippled, broken edge to the left. Inside the shape does not matter. Push paint in firm, then scrape the excess off firmly.
- placement: peak at x ~0.45-0.5, top at about y 0.3 at this stage; right tail reaches x ~0.9, left side runs to x ~0.05-0.1 at the horizon. Base sits just above the horizon (y ~0.5).
- result: dark jagged hard-edged silhouette.
- rule: the only concern is the clean outer edge [08:50]
- seen: 08:31, 08:42, 08:53, 09:04

### 09:13 Mountain: pull down with the brush
- tool: 2 in brush, clean and dry (?).
- motion: grab the knife paint at the outer edge and pull downward following the slope direction, then blend the base with horizontal passes so the paint fades away at the haze/horizon. The left tail is smeared sideways at the slope angle. Firmer at the start, lifting off as the stroke goes down.
- result: soft dark mountain, crisp top edge, lighter toward the base, a pale mist band forming at its foot.
- rule: the pull removes the extra paint and lets the base get lighter [09:19]
- seen: 09:15, 09:21, 09:27, 09:33, 09:39, 09:45

### 09:40 Snow highlights (light side)
- tool: large palette knife.
- load: titanium white spread flat, cut across for a small roll at the edge.
- motion: point of the knife set on the ridge line, then pulled downward along the slope, short strokes ~0.5-1 in, light pressure so the white breaks up over the dark.
- placement: right-hand side of each ridge facet (light from the right), from the peak down along the right slope toward the right tail; more at the peak, thinner lower. First band from the peak (x ~0.47) to about x ~0.62.
- result: broken, stippled white strokes along the right edge of the dark peak.
- rule: decide where the light would strike the mountain [10:13]
- seen: 09:51 (palette), 09:57, 10:07, 10:12

### 10:36 Snow: knife edge
- tool: same knife, blade stood on its edge (roughly perpendicular to the canvas).
- motion: edge dragged downward along the flanks; a small amount of snow goes in tight spots, and the right face filled out. The mountain becomes more faceted: a second smaller peak left of the main one, another small peak on the right.
- seen: 10:18, 10:24, 10:30, 10:33

### 10:43 Shadows and structure
- tool: small palette knife.
- load: phthalo blue + titanium white (? about 1:2; pale grey-blue), small roll on the edge.
- motion: zero pressure, barely touch so the paint breaks and shows holes of the dark below; short pull downward/sideways along the facet; repeat. Follow the slope angles. Play highlight and shadow back and forth: blue shadow on the left/under side of each ridge and in the valleys, white against the right side; a valley comes around on the left, a ridge sweeps out and bring it back in [11:41-12:23].
- placement: left faces of the peaks, the center fold of the main ridge, the valley on the left of the main mass; the small foothill peak at left (x ~0.15-0.3) gets a lit white face with its own blue shadow.
- result: faceted snow mountain, grainy lit right faces, blue-grey stripes on the left faces, dark rock showing in cracks, lighter toward the base. Left side ends in a dark foothill.
- rule: watch the angles very closely [11:30]
- seen: 11:08, 11:15, 11:22, 11:30, 11:45, 12:09, 12:30

### 12:54 Mist at the mountain base
- tool: large clean dry brush (2 in (?)).
- motion: tapping, following the slope angles; on the left side tap leftward, on the right a little this way, each tap with a small lift; then pull together horizontally in the middle.
- placement: lowest part of the mountain, y ~0.45-0.55, covering the bottom of the snow and the dark foothill.
- result: soft white-blue haze, the base dissolves into the horizon.
- seen: 13:06, 13:14, 13:22

### 13:35 Evergreens
- tool: fan brush, held by the handle end, corner of the fan on the canvas.
- load: leftover mountain mix (black, Prussian blue, Van Dyke, crimson) + touch of sap green, a big pile; fan loaded very full.
- motion: touch the corner, push the paint out sideways/downward in small taps: narrow pointed top, branch tiers widening downward. Then pile on dark taps to make a continuous mass and decide the tops afterward by pushing up from it. Far trees: grab and pull up so they are small and distant.
- placement: first tree on the right (x ~0.85-0.9, top at y ~0.35); then dark bands at the horizon (y ~0.5-0.6): left x 0.0-0.35 up to the mountain tail, right x 0.6-1.0. Tops highest at the outer right (to y ~0.1-0.15), lower toward the middle. Little tops just here and there.
- result: dark masses with ragged stippled lower edges that fall into the blue (reflection-like).
- rule: there is no need for much detail; a few small hints are enough [16:12]
- seen: 14:21, 14:34, 14:47, 15:14, 15:28, 15:42, 15:56, 16:21, 16:32, 16:43, 16:54, 17:05

### 18:15 Land: dark banks
- tool: 2 in brush, flat, tapping straight down.
- load: same dark mix, more blue/black/brown/green mixed on the brush.
- motion: tap downward (not drag), irregular blobs, ragged edges; the lower edge hangs down into the water as a stippled fringe.
- placement: left bank x 0.0-0.4, y ~0.55-0.85; right bank x 0.5-1.0, y ~0.6-0.95, larger toward the bottom right. The middle (x 0.3-0.7) stays blue as the stream and widens toward the viewer.
- rule: show that the land sits higher than the water [19:45]; dark is needed for light to read [18:34]
- seen: 18:15, 18:35, 18:55, 19:15, 19:35, 19:55

### 20:05 Tree trunks
- tool: palette knife (blade size ?), edge.
- load: dark sienna + white + touch of blue, spread flat, cut across for a thin roll.
- motion: edge cuts through the dark tree masses in thin, slightly wobbly vertical lines, light touch; some strokes scrape down to the canvas color (the canvas takes off whatever paint it wants).
- placement: inside the tree clusters, not in every tree, mostly mid-height down.
- result: faint grey-brown trunks and twig impressions in the dark green.
- rule: cut through the paint and let some of the bare canvas show through [20:31]
- seen: 20:16, 20:27, 20:38, 20:49, 21:00

### 21:36 Tree highlights
- tool: same unwashed brush with blue and green still on it (? the frames show a thin-handled brush at the trees).
- load: add cadmium yellow into the dirty brush for dark green.
- motion: touch and push downward a little, a few spots only; also on the big, aged tree.
- rule: evergreens usually look quite dark [22:07]
- seen: 21:55, 22:05, 22:15, 22:25

### 22:36 Grass on the banks
- tool: 2 in brush, tapping downward.
- load: cad yellow, sap green, yellow ochre, touch of Indian yellow, mixed on the brush; the load runs out as you tap.
- motion: gentle taps down on top of the dark banks: first the far bank band just under the tree line (left x 0.2-0.45, y ~0.55-0.6), then successive mounds in front, each plane a little lighter/yellower than the one behind; rounded humps with lit upper edges and dark below; repeated tapping makes it gentler. Shapes follow the lay of the land.
- placement: left mounds stacked from y ~0.55 to ~0.9, right mounds the same; the stream stays between them, S-shaped, narrowing toward the horizon at x ~0.6-0.7.
- rule: begin with the background and move forward [25:19]; for a new plane, lighten the color slightly and drop it in [25:06]
- seen: 22:48, 23:01, 23:59, 24:18, 24:37, 24:56, 25:15, 25:30

### 23:14 Bushes
- tool: 1 in brush, pushed on, corner.
- load: same yellow-greens.
- motion: push in vertical clumps against the base of the trees: 2-3 small bushes on the left (x ~0.25-0.4, y ~0.5-0.58) and 2-3 on the right (x ~0.65-0.9, y ~0.45-0.55).
- result: small bright yellow-green shrubs between the dark trees and the grass.
- seen: 23:14, 23:27, 23:40

### 24:10 Red sparkle
- Every so often a touch of bright red on the brush while tapping the grass, to brighten it [24:14]; amount very small, visible as warmer spots in the mounds (?).

### 25:36 Water: reflections
- tool: 1 in brush (dirty, grass color).
- motion: pull straight down from the bank edge into the water, light pressure, then sweep across horizontally to soften.
- placement: under the lower edge of each bank, left and right, short pulls (1-2 in).
- seen: 25:43, 25:51, 25:59, 26:07

### 26:15 Stream highlights
- tool: second fan brush, dipped in Liquid White first, then titanium white.
- load: full of titanium white, least touch of phthalo blue (pale blue-white).
- motion: scrub across in basically straight horizontal lines; where a streak meets a bank let it pick up the green and blend. This cuts off the bottom of the land masses.
- placement: a thin streak near the far horizon (y ~0.57), mid streaks (y ~0.7-0.8), a pale band across the bottom of the left bank (y ~0.88).
- result: pale ripples; bank bottoms end on a straight line; water lightest at the far end.
- rule: keep stream lines fairly straight; they set where the land masses end [26:49]
- seen: 26:29, 26:43, 26:57, 27:11, 27:25

## Rules and principles he states
- [01:06] Liquid White makes the canvas wet and slick so colors blend.
- [02:25] Sky blended downward gets lighter toward the horizon: depth and distance.
- [04:09] Thin paint sticks to thick paint; start thick and firm, add thinner layers on top. Dry brush for blending.
- [05:21] Clouds are free: drop them in, do not overwork.
- [06:24] Blend only the cloud bases, never the top; tiny circles.
- [07:18] Fluff cloud tops in circles, never straight up.
- [08:05] Keep crimson low in the mountain mix unless you want purple mountains.
- [08:50] For the mountain only the outside edge matters; pull down to lighten the base.
- [10:13] Snow goes where the light hits (light from the right).
- [11:07] Shadow knife: no pressure so the paint breaks.
- [11:30] Pay close attention to the angles of the mountain.
- [16:55] Pulling distant trees up makes them look farther away.
- [18:34] Dark first: you need dark to show light.
- [19:32] Water lies in a recessed area; the land must be higher than the water.
- [22:07] Do not overhighlight evergreens.
- [23:57] Different planes show depth; lighten each plane as you come forward.
- [25:19] Start with what is farthest away and work forward.
- [26:49] Keep stream lines basically straight.

## Finished painting
- focal point: the snow peak at about x 0.47, y 0.17 (top), lit on its right faces; the mountain spans x ~0.3-0.75, base y ~0.45.
- far: mountain with hazy base, sky and clouds. middle: dark evergreens at the horizon on both sides with yellow-green bushes (left x 0.1-0.25, right x 0.8-0.9, y ~0.5). near: rolling grass banks left and right, lightest and yellowest at the bottom right.
- water: S-curved stream from the bottom left (x ~0.05-0.45, y 1.0) up through the center and off to the right at the horizon (x ~0.6-0.75, y ~0.58); about 25% of the canvas. Sky with mountain about 40% (top), land about 35%.
- darkest darks: the shaded tree masses and bank at left (x 0-0.3, y 0.4-0.7) and the trees at far right; lightest lights: snow on the right of the peak, the centers of the upper right cumulus, then the pale streaks in the water.
- framing: tall evergreens left (x 0-0.3) and right (x 0.85-1.0), dark blue sky in the top corners.
- light from the right; cool palette: blue sky and water, grey-blue mountain, green banks with yellow highlights.

## Surprises
- The stream is the blue slab painted with the sky mix at 02:42 and kept; the dark land is placed around it afterwards, and the only late water work is the white streaks.
- The sky is blended dry BEFORE the clouds go in.
- The mountain is dropped in with the knife in a few strokes, the inside scraped off, and the form comes from the 2 in brush pull-down; snow is added with the knife point pulled downward, shadows with a second, small knife.
- Tree trunks are scratched out with a knife through the wet dark paint, not painted.
- The first quarter of the episode (to 03:30) paints the water, not the sky only.
