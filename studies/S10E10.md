# S10E10 Ocean Sunset
- palette: cadmium yellow, yellow ochre, bright red, alizarin crimson, phthalo blue, titanium white, dark sienna, Van Dyke brown. Phthalo green is mentioned as a possible extra for seas, not used (10:43).
- canvas: 18x24 in, landscape. Split coat: the top ~44% (down to the horizon line, y ~0.44) has a thin even coat of Liquid White, the lower ~56% has Liquid Clear (00:14-00:35). Nothing pre-painted.
- light: low sun glowing behind the sky, centre-left (x ~0.3-0.55, y ~0.1-0.35). Warm sunset sky, cool dark sea; the wave face is back-lit and glows yellow-green. Dark purple cloud mass upper right, red at the far left horizon.
- positions are fractions of the canvas, x from the left edge, y from the top. Close-up frames are zoomed and the canvas is photographed at an angle, so values are approximate (?).

## Steps
### 00:37 Sky: yellow, ochre, red
- tool: 2 in brush, flat, hair against the canvas.
- load: a very small amount of cad yellow beaten into the bristles; then, with no cleaning, yellow ochre loaded the same way; then bright red (strong, a little goes a long way) (01:33).
- motion: small criss-cross X strokes, light; each new colour goes around the outside of the previous one so they mix on the wet Liquid White. A few strokes may drift below the horizon.
- placement: yellow in the centre of the sky (x ~0.2-0.6, y ~0.1-0.35), ochre around it, red as a ring along the horizon (y ~0.3-0.42) and at the left edge (x 0-0.2). A band of red also runs along the bottom of the sky at the horizon.
- result: orange-yellow glow with a red lower band; light blends softly because the coat is wet.
- seen: 01:16, 02:08
### 02:23 Sky: white core
- tool: a second, clean brush (several brushes in rotation so the washing is kept down).
- load: straight titanium white.
- motion: set the white in the brightest area of the glow, then grab it and pull outward.
- placement: centre of the sun glow, about x 0.4-0.55, y ~0.2-0.3 (?).
- rule: light on light barely shows now, it pays off after the pull [02:45]; whatever lands below the horizon is covered later [03:02]
- seen: 03:00
### 03:19 Clouds: reddish purple
- tool: 2 in brush after cleaning; only the corner touches.
- load: little phthalo, then alizarin crimson; far more crimson than blue so the colour is on the red side; very little paint on the brush.
- motion: little circular strokes with the corner, building rounded cloud shapes; place where it looks right, number is free.
- placement: big mass upper right (x 0.55-1.0, y 0-0.35), smaller ones left of the glow (x 0.1-0.35, y 0.1-0.25) and a low band along the horizon (?).
- result: mauve-purple clouds against the yellow.
- seen: 04:04, 04:58
### 04:54 Clouds: blend
- tool: clean dry brush (2 in (?)), corner.
- motion: small round strokes, softening the edges only; then three hairs and some air, a barely-there pass that drags the colour across so it looks like thin streaks of light passing through.
- rule: soften, do not blend the shapes away [05:15]
- seen: 05:52
### 05:49 Nearer cloud
- tool: same brush, then clean dry brush.
- load: the same crimson + phthalo, a little darker and a little more on the brush.
- motion: lay the darker colour in a cloud inside the mass, then blend with a very light circular pass.
- placement: inside the right-centre mass (x ~0.6-0.85, y ~0.15-0.3) (?).
- rule: a darker cloud reads as closer, so value alone sets depth in the sky [06:11, 06:47]
- seen: 06:46, 07:40
### 08:31 Layout sketch
- tool: filbert, loaded with paint thinned to the thickness of water (thinner added).
- motion: draw the line, not a commitment, it can be moved. Crest line: from the left edge (x ~0.02, y ~0.60) rising in a smooth arc to the peak at x ~0.47, y ~0.50, then dropping down to the right. A second line comes back under it and loops up at the right into a hook (x ~0.8, y ~0.48) for the crash, so the crash is a curl over to the right. Shoreline: a wavy line along the lower part, from x ~0.04, y ~0.78 across to x ~0.96, y ~0.75 with a small rise at the right.
- result: thin dark outline on the grey Liquid Clear.
- rule: a guide saves trouble at first, less needed with practice [09:21]
- seen: 09:06, 09:20
### 09:30 Sea block-in
- tool: 2 in brush, flat.
- load: dark lavender, more crimson and phthalo added as it runs out; paint goes on thick.
- motion: horizontal side-to-side passes; the guide lines are followed around, so the colour wraps the outside of the crest.
- placement: far-sea band right under the sky, y 0.45-0.55 across the full width, thickest at the right; second band from the shoreline up, y ~0.63-0.84, x 0-1, with a rising dark belly under the wave; the pale wave face is left open between them: y ~0.55-0.65 on the left, widening into the crest zone at the middle and the crash at the right. Below y ~0.84 the clear coat stays bare for the beach.
- result: two dark navy-lavender bands with a pale gap.
- rule: because the lower coat is Liquid Clear the dark keeps its value; Liquid White would pick up the white and weaken it [09:35-09:55]. All that is wanted here is dark so the light shows later [10:25].
- seen: 09:37, 10:08, 10:39, 11:10, 11:30
### 11:22 Headlands

- load: dark lavender (crimson + phthalo).
- motion: tap and drag the corner of the fan along the horizon; one small shape, then a bigger one in front of it. Little highlight added only if the shape does not read [12:29].
- placement: right end of the horizon, x ~0.75-1.0, y 0.40-0.50; the far one is lower and lighter (the pointed hill top rising to the right edge), the second is bigger, darker and sits in front of it. Too far away for detail, only the basic outline.
- result: a dark rising mass at the right horizon edge with a stippled, grainy top.
- rule: another plane in the distance helps distance, trees can go on them [12:09]
- seen: 11:54, 12:10, 12:26, 12:42, 15:02

- placement: from the lower band (y ~0.68) up to the crest (y ~0.5), fanning in from both sides.

## Rules and principles he states
- [00:20] The split coat: Liquid White for the sky, Liquid Clear for the sea so dark colours stay dark [09:35].
- [01:24] Strokes that stray into the sea area do not matter, they are covered.
- [03:44] Clouds: corner of the brush, tiny circles, count and place at will.
- [05:17] Blend only enough to soften the edges, keep the shapes.
- [06:11] A darker cloud looks closer: depth in the sky.
- [08:36] A thinned-paint sketch of crest, crash and shore saves trouble at first.
- [10:25] Dark first, so the light parts show later.
- [12:21] Distant headlands add planes and distance.
- [14:41] Watch the angles; dark areas matter more than light here.
- [16:05] Frame first (dark), highlights second.
- [16:33] Stroke direction is very important; wipe the brush between strokes so it does not pick up the underpaint.
- [17:29] Light sits along the wave top because water is thinner there.
- [19:48] Keep the highlight colour clean by wiping instead of washing.
- [22:15] Paint for the sparkles is as thin as ink.
- [24:12] Add dark at the edges so the light looks stronger.
- [24:58] Leave dark areas in the foam, do not cover them all.

## Finished painting

- far: warm sunset sky (top ~44%), dark headlands at right (x 0.75-1.0, y 0.4-0.5), thin far swells. middle: the big wave with the lit face. near: dark navy foreground water with curved pale streaks, then the beach strip (y ~0.82-1.0).
- darkest darks: the far-sea band at the horizon on the right, behind the crash, and the lower band under the wave (y ~0.65-0.8). Lightest lights: the splash fringe on the crash, the crest glow, the sky glow at x 0.45, y 0.25.
- proportions: sky ~44%, sea ~38%, beach ~18% (?).
- light: from behind (sun at centre-left), so the wave is back-lit and its face glows. Palette: warm yellow-red-orange sky and sand against cold navy-lavender sea; the glow colours repeat in the wave face and the sand.

## Surprises
- Two bases: Liquid White on the sky, Liquid Clear on the sea. The sea is painted thick, dark and unblended into white, so the dark stays dark.
- A thin-paint line sketch of the wave and shoreline on the clear coat before any sea paint.
- The wave is built by value only: dark bands first, the face left bare, then light and glow scrubbed in, then dark pulled back in with a dry brush.
- The splash is made with an upward push of a full filbert, not by dabbing.
- The beach is an orange-yellow-red strip of bright colour laid on the bare clear coat and blended horizontally; it reads as reflection of the sky.
- Sky is done first and in warm colours; sky clouds are crimson-based purple, not white.
