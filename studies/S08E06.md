# S08E06 Bubbling Mountain Brook
- palette: titanium white, liquid white (mixed into the water white as thinner), Indian yellow, bright red, alizarin crimson, phthalo blue, midnight black, dark sienna, Van Dyke brown, sap green, yellow ochre, cadmium yellow (?)
- canvas: thin even coat of liquid white, wet and slick. Nothing pre-painted. Landscape format. The lower blue band of the sky stage becomes the lake and the brook bed.
- light: warm sun glow behind the clouds, upper center (x~0.45, y~0.2); backlit warm yellow-orange-pink sky, cool blue-lavender mountains and water. Slope and rock highlights go on the sun-facing slopes (?).

## Steps
### 00:24 Sky glow
- tool: 2 in brush, flat face on canvas
- load: tiniest touch of Indian yellow + bright red mixed on the brush, the smallest amount possible; a little more red added at 00:43 (reads orange)
- motion: short bouncy horizontal strokes, wandering left and right, paint dragged out sideways at both ends (drifting away to the side)
- placement: horizontal blob across upper middle, y ~0.10-0.40, x ~0.1-0.95, ragged ends, rising toward the right; white canvas everywhere else
- result: soft orange core in a yellow band on white
- seen: 00:54, 01:11, 02:20

### 01:04 Wash brush
- wash in odorless thinner and wipe before the blue.

### 01:13 Sky blue and black
- tool: same 2 in brush
- load: midnight black tapped in, a little phthalo blue; midnight black lets a lot of light through
- motion: horizontal sweeps along the top, working around the glow, never over it (no wish to cover up this bright color); blue and black in scattered spots
- placement: top ~35% of canvas, strongest in the top-left corner and along the top edge, thinning toward the glow; also reaches the left edge beside the glow
- result: dark blue-grey top, yellow-orange glow below, white still showing underneath
- seen: 01:28, 01:45

### 02:04 Lake blue
- tool: 2 in brush, same blue/black
- load: same, a little thinner (a small splash of water)
- motion: horizontal side-to-side strokes, flat brush
- placement: band y ~0.62-0.88, almost full width (x ~0.1-0.95); a white gap (y ~0.45-0.60) stays between glow and band
- result: dark steel-blue band; later mountain-base mist and brook water
- seen: 02:20, 02:32

### 02:26 Blend and red scrub
- tool: clean 2 in brush
- motion: very gentle blending of the edges where blue meets glow, light horizontal strokes, with no need to blend heavily; the lower blue band is also brushed horizontally (streak texture shows at 14:00)
- 02:40 load: small amount of bright red on the 2 in, scrub it firmly into the canvas (cross scrub), centre of glow
- placement: red in the glow centre, y ~0.25-0.35, x ~0.3-0.8
- result: red-orange core, yellow around it
- seen: 02:46, 02:53, 03:00

### 03:02 Clouds
- tool: 1 in brush for the circles; 2 in top corner to blend and fluff
- load: titanium white with a touch of bright red (pink tinge, make the clouds glow); thick in places, thin or bare in others
- motion: very small circles, brush always moving, scrubbed into a cloud with scalloped top; circles kept small. Then 2 in top corner: mix and blend, then fluff (tap and lift) so any stringy pull-ups blend away; work it loose, like backcombing hair. More red into the white for the next cloud. Finally blend the whole sky very gently (05:17)
- placement: along the top of the glow, y ~0.05-0.40, several clouds across x ~0.1-0.95 billowing up above the orange band; one low cloud drifts down at centre; glow shows through under them
- result: pink-white cumulus with orange-pink underside
- rule: build in layers, starting with the most distant one [04:15]
- seen: 03:26, 04:00, 04:34, 04:51, 05:08

### 05:28 Mountain 1 (back range)
- tool: painting knife for the top edge; 2 in brush to pull down
- load: black + phthalo blue + alizarin crimson; test with a dab of white, push it to purple (add a bit more crimson). Flattened on palette, slice off a small roll of paint on the knife edge (05:42-05:56: dark roll, lavender once tuned)
- motion: knife held nearly upright, long edge on canvas, tip up; draws the ridgeline left to right in several drags, tilting to make peaks and drops; paint lands only on the top edge (the only concern is a clean top edge)
- placement: ridge starts ~x 0.2, extends to x ~0.9; summit centre-right; top edge y ~0.30-0.33; laid over the yellow glow (lavender + yellow = brown)
- result: dark purple jagged line, no body yet
- rule: lavender over yellow makes a pleasant brown; too much blue gives green mountains [06:15-06:28]
- 06:47 pull-down: 2 in brush, pull down with a very light touch: long vertical strokes from the edge down, directions following the slopes you plan (diagonal where a ridge runs); base fades into the pale mist at y ~0.55-0.6; several repeats lay out the ridges and valleys
- seen: 05:42, 05:56, 06:10, 06:24, 06:38, 07:06, 07:20

### 07:42 Mountain 1 highlights and shadows

- motion: edge barely grazes the canvas (floating across, almost weightless); start at the peak and pull down along the slope in short strokes, knife angle matching the slope; leaves a broken, textured streak (canvas tooth shows). 08:12-08:24: one band from the summit steeply down to the right (~60-70 deg from horizontal), then parallel bands beside it
- load 2: the mountain mix + white (darker than highlight, lighter than base); tapped into the highlights; the least paint possible for the shadow areas
- load 3 (09:30): crimson + white = warm pink; shiny highlights, layer after layer, bumps along ridges, like small rocks found up high
- load 4 (10:44): phthalo blue (tiniest) + white with the knife tip, touched along the ridge edges (it makes it glitter)
- placement: sun-facing slopes of each peak; pink body of the range, blue-white at ridge crests; shadow sides stay lavender-purple
- result: pink and white faceted slopes with darker valleys between
- rule: angles make all the difference [09:17]; consider where the light would hit [10:25]
- seen: 07:57, 08:15, 08:32, 09:07, 09:42, 10:00, 10:21, 10:32, 10:43, 11:06

## Rules and principles he states
- [00:35] very small amount of paint for the sky glow.
- [01:24] midnight black is transparent, lets the glow show.
- [04:15] clouds: work in layers, most distant first.
- [04:02] fluff lifts clouds; stringy bits vanish when blended.
- [06:15] lavender over yellow makes brown; too much blue gives green mountains.
- [06:40] only the mountain top edge matters.
- [08:09] highlights barely touch the canvas.
- [09:17] angles are the secret; follow the angles.
- [09:21] smallest amount of paint for shadows.
- [10:25] think where the light would strike.
- [11:23] tap and lift upward to push things back.
- [12:16] each mountain forward should be darker.
- [15:40] each plane adds distance.
- [17:37] water paint thinner than the wet paint under it or it turns to mud.
- [19:13] dark in first so the light shows.
- [19:59] water is lazy; stones explain a fall; it never climbs.
- [21:56] far things dark; leave some trees without highlight [22:16].
- [23:41] anything unwanted can be covered.

## Finished painting
- focal point: the fall and the central peak. Fall x~0.48, y~0.75; main peak x~0.50, y~0.27.
- planes: far = sky, clouds (y 0-0.4) and main range (peaks y ~0.27-0.55, pink/blue facets, mist base); middle = range 2 at left (x 0-0.55, y 0.45-0.66), humps, lake, firs (x 0.68-0.75, y 0.45-0.70); near = brook with white bands, brown slabs, yellow bushes, orange bush, big pine at right.
- darkest darks: firs, left evergreen, banks under the bushes, slabs flanking the fall. Lightest: cloud tops, white water bands bottom centre, pale glow behind the peak.
- framing: pine at right (x ~0.93), left fir (x ~0.04), right bank mass, yellow bushes lower left.
- proportions: sky ~35%, mountain/mist ~25%, water + banks ~40%.
- palette temperature: warm sky and autumn foliage, cool lavender mountains and water.

## Surprises
- The sky starts as a warm glow with blue/black only around it; no blue sky coat first.
- The lake blue goes in with the sky, before any mountain; the brook is later drawn in white straight onto it, no separate water base.
- Mountains: knife lays the top edge, 2 in pulls it down, all highlights with the knife.
- Fan brush paints all the water (lip, fall, splash, foam bands); the evergreens are pushed upward.
- Dark banks are pounced in with the 2 in before the firs and bushes; the big pine is the last thing painted.
