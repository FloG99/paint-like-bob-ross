# Streams, brooks, waterfalls

> Moving water is the base blue left showing, with white pulled over it last. It's never painted as a blue object on top of the land. Falls are a sheet pulled down from a lip, held back by rocks, with mist where they land.

Counts (N/M): episodes whose narration names the step / episodes that paint the subject. They are lower bounds: not everything done is said.

## Stream or brook
Three ways seen. In all of them the water's color goes in first or is the dark base, and the land is shaped around it. In the dataset (26 episodes with a stream) the fan is the usual tool (16/26, knife 2/26), the paint is white (21/26), often thinned with Liquid White or Magic White (thinning named 12/26, Liquid White/Clear 9/26).
1. **Stream kept from the water slab** [S11E01]:
   1. Right after the sky, scrub a dark blue slab across the lower part with the sky brush (horizontal back-and-forth).
   2. Later, tap the dark land banks in from both sides so the middle stays blue, S-shaped, narrowing toward the horizon and widening toward the viewer.
   3. Reflections: pull straight down from the banks, then across.
   4. Last: with a fan dipped in Liquid White and then titanium white (+ the least phthalo), scrub straight horizontal streaks. These cut off the bottoms of the banks. Keep these lines basically straight.
2. **Stream on a dark underpainting** [S15E08 18:24]:
   1. The whole lower canvas is already dark.
   2. Lay the bank edge first (a wavy knife line of dark brown, with brown + white pebbles on top).
   3. Then the stream is only thin curved horizontal pulls of white thinned with Liquid White, plus short broken dashes. The dark between them is the water. It widens toward the viewer.
3. **Brook over a lake band** [S08E06 17:22, 24:47]:
   1. The water's blue went in with the sky.
   2. With a fan, Liquid White and then white (thinner than the paint under it, or it turns to mud), make one long flat pull for the lip, a short rounded drop below it, and a stipple of foam taps at its foot.
   3. Below that, add curved bands (concave up) with ragged scalloped lower edges that widen toward the viewer and swirl around rocks. Leave dark water between them.

**Rules**
- Water always takes the easy way down: it never climbs (a flat, level pull is stated in 6/26 [S06E07 13:44, S05E13 19:31]). Every drop needs a stone to explain it.
- Stones hold the falls back (rock/stone named in 10/43 waterfall steps, 2/26 stream steps). [S08E06 19:59, S17E10 23:47]
- Dark underneath so the water shows (4/26): [S03E03 12:10, S05E13 19:31]. Thin white sticks to thick paint (S03E03 10:08).
- Lines stay straight or gently curved and level (straight 3/26).
- The water is lightest at the far end and where it falls.

## Waterfall
Most falls in the dataset are small (30/43 episodes say small, tiny, little or thin): a short drop of thin white over a ledge in the middle ground [S19E13 20:31, S13E06 17:55, S02E11 06:28, S10E03 22:00]. The big-sheet method below is the less common one.
1. **Rocks first**:
   - Lay the cliffs on both sides dark (fan or knife, Van Dyke + dark sienna + crimson), shaped vertically.
   - Lights: marbled crimson + blue + white, or dark sienna, put on by skipping and bouncing the knife along the rock's angle.
   - Blend with a dry brush in the direction of the rock, never across. Leave these rocks quite dark.
   - [S17E07 09:25, 14:00]
2. **Sheet**:
   - Use a 1" brush (or a fan) dipped in Liquid Clear or Liquid White, then titanium white. (fan 29/43, 2" 8/43, 1" 2/43; Liquid or Magic White 26/43; white 38/43; blue in the mix 26/43) [S19E13 20:31, S07E13 13:57, S01E05 07:35]
   - Set the bristle edge at the lip and pull straight down, light, so the bristles bend. (pull or drop down 28/43; the lip, edge or ledge named 15/43; a push or bend upward at the top for foam 15/43) [S10E04 11:35, S03E01 08:09, S07E06 12:19]
   - Start next to one rock and add strokes side by side, one brush width each. The streaks stay visible (4/43 describe separate strokes). [S02E12 08:30]
   - Use the most paint at the top where the light hits, fading lower so the dark shows (thin, fade or lighter 22/43 [S17E10 22:33, S02E11 06:28]).
   - The lip is a rounded shoulder; the strokes curl over it.
   - [S17E07 11:44]
3. **Lip stone**: one small dark stone on the edge, with a dot of light on top. [S17E07 12:40]
4. **Mist at the foot**: with the 2" top corner and a little white, tap a ragged cloud bank over the ends of the strokes, then soften it upward. Water falling this far always throws up a lot of mist. Smaller falls get a foam or splash dab instead (mist, spray, splash or foam named in 21/43 waterfall steps). [S17E07 17:24, S02E11 06:53, S07E13 14:37, S01E05 11:02]
5. **Pool**: horizontal bands of Liquid Clear + white coming out of the mist. Water wraps around a stone in the pool. For splashes, push up so the bristles bend upward. Add ripples toward the viewer. [S17E07 18:26, 19:41]
6. **Small fall in a bank**:
   - A short pale vertical drop (white + Liquid White + a trace of blue), widening at the bottom, in a gap of the bank.
   - A rock in front of it pushes it back.
   - Add little white splash dabs at its base.
   - [S19E13 20:54, 21:32]

## Variants across episodes
- **Thin white from the fan, dropped or pulled over the edge** with no rocks painted first: the common small fall (fan 29/43). [S07E13 13:57, S01E05 07:35, S02E11 06:28]
- **Fall run over a stone edge in sienna** (thin paint runs down): stones hold it back. [S17E10 23:47]
- **Oval or round brush** bubbling the water down over a dark underlayer. [S10E04 11:35]
- **Stream wandering behind a rock** and out again, closer water drawn larger. (rock 2/26 stream steps) [S09E13 15:26, 16:04]
- **Dark stream line first** (green + crimson, across), then fan highlights thinned with Magic White. [S05E13 19:31, 19:57]

## Proportions
- **Big falls**: about 0.4 of the width and 0.25 of the height of a portrait canvas, in the middle third, under the mountain. [S17E07]
- **Small falls**: an inch tall, in the middle ground. [S19E13]
- **Streams**: enter at the bottom, 0.3–0.5 of the width, and narrow to a thread at the horizon.

## Mistakes
- White as thick as (or thicker than) the paint under it.
- Blue painted on top of the finished land.
- Rocks lit all over.
- No mist where water lands.
- Water flowing without stones to explain it.
