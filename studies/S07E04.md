# S07E04 Mountain Cabin
- palette: titanium white, midnight black, phthalo blue, phthalo green, Van Dyke brown, dark sienna, yellow ochre, cadmium yellow, bright red, sap green; liquid white (base coat, water line, highlights), liquid black (limbs). Title cards also list alizarin crimson (not clearly used).
- canvas: thin even coat of Liquid White, wet and slick. Nothing pre-painted.
- light: bright day, big cumulus sky, hazy mist at the mountain foot. Right-facing mountain slopes are the lit warm-orange faces, left faces are blue-gray shadow, so light comes from the upper right (?). Cool sky/water, warm orange mountain and bushes.

## Steps
### 00:19 Sky: blue criss-cross
- tool: 2-in brush, flat face to canvas.
- load: midnight black + phthalo blue mixed on the brush by tapping into the bristles (mostly blue, black darkens it, ~3:1 (?)).
- motion: small criss-cross X strokes, bouncing, light-medium pressure, ~2-4 in per stroke (?).
- placement: top ~35-40% of canvas, full width; white canvas holes left deliberately.
- result: blotchy mid-blue sky with white gaps (00:42, 00:52).
- rule: keep some bare gaps of canvas showing in the sky [00:39]
- seen: 00:33, 00:42, 00:52

### 00:52 Water: green-blue pulls
- tool: same dirty 2-in brush, no wash.
- load: adds a small amount of phthalo green, tapped in; teal.
- motion: horizontal strokes pulled from the outside edges in toward the middle (from left edge and from right edge).
- placement: below the horizon (~y 0.55-1.0); a light unpainted area left near the centre as the sheen of light.
- result: horizontal teal band, pale gap left of centre at 01:10; teal on both sides by 01:20.
- rule: leave a small area open as a sheen of light [01:12]
- seen: 01:10, 01:20

### 01:25 Blend sky and water
- tool: brush scrubbed in thinner and shaken; clean 2-in brush.
- motion: sky: soft strokes working down, blending only a little to keep the little actions. Water: very light horizontal strokes across; the light area stays where placed.
- result: soft textured sky above, smooth horizontal water, pale horizon haze between (02:04, 02:15).
- rule: blending too much turns the sky into a flat, lifeless blue [01:47]
- seen: 01:42, 01:53, 02:04, 02:15

### 02:27 Clouds
- tool: 2-in brush, cleaned and shaken; pulled in one direction through white to load.
- load: titanium white + a little red (tops read pinkish); a lot of paint in the bristles.
- motion: dancing, jiggling, small curls; constantly moving; paint thick in places, thin in others, none in others.
- placement: upper middle and right of the sky, in front of the blue.
- result: bumpy white-pink cloud tops with blue holes (02:53).
- rule: keep the brush moving, or the cloud turns into a solid lump [02:54]
- seen: 02:39, 02:53

### 03:08 Cloud blend and fluff
- tool: same brush, cleaned, dry.
- motion: gentle mixing motion on the underside only (like beating cake batter), not touching the top edge; removes excess paint. Then lift/fluff upward, drag out stringy bits and leave them; the blend of the sky afterwards brings it together.
- result: puffy cumulus, soft bottoms melting into pale horizon (03:50, 04:05).
- seen: 03:07, 03:22, 03:50

### 04:05 Far hill
- tool: palette knife for shape, dirty 2-in brush for slopes.
- load: blue + black + white, blue-gray; knife loaded by cutting off a bit of the mix.
- motion: knife lays a small peaked shape; brush strokes then run down the slopes (diagonal, light) for highlight and shadow; light blend over the whole hill; tap the ends so they disappear.
- placement: right of the later main mountain, peak ~x 0.65, y 0.3, overlapping the cloud bottoms.
- result: faint soft hill (04:42).
- rule: the far hill should be very soft and faint, far in the distance [04:40]
- seen: 04:28, 04:35, 04:42, 04:50

### 05:00 Main mountain mass
- tool: palette knife, edge.
- load: black + touch phthalo blue + Van Dyke brown mixed on the palette; pulled out and cut across for a roll of paint on the knife edge.
- motion: short jagged strokes drawing a pointed peak with a ridge falling to the left and a wing to the right; edges follow slope angles; pressed hard into the canvas.
- placement: peak ~x 0.38, y 0.35; left wing out to the left edge, right wing to the far hill.
- result: dark, ragged, white flecks showing (05:21, 05:35).
- rule: press the paint firmly into the canvas [05:38]
- seen: 05:07, 05:21, 05:35, 05:45

### 05:51 Soften mountain
- tool: 2-in brush, flat, dry.
- motion: pull down gently from the ridge along slope direction (diagonal strokes), light pressure; tap outer ends so they disappear; horizontal sweeps at the base to dissolve into mist.
- result: smooth blue-gray slopes with fine stripes, black peak top intact (06:10, 06:20).
- rule: pulling down removes extra paint so the next layer will adhere [06:00]
- seen: 05:55, 06:05, 06:15

### 06:33 Highlight on the farthest peak
- tool: palette knife.
- load: dark sienna, Van Dyke, white, bright red; unmixed streaks; small roll on the edge.
- motion: knife edge laid on the slope, tiny dabs, no stretching.
- placement: thin streak along the right-facing slope of the middle peak.
- result: thin orange-brown speckled line (07:00, 07:10).
- rule: use the smallest possible amount of paint [07:00]
- seen: 06:50, 07:00, 07:10

### 07:10 Shadow side
- tool: palette knife, then brush.
- load: white + blue + black + a little brown, blue-gray.
- motion: knife lays shadow; tap with the brush; then barely touch, following the slope angles, fading to nothing.
- placement: left faces of the peaks.
- rule: barely graze the canvas, following the slope angles [07:55]
- seen: 07:21, 07:32, 07:53, 08:04

### 08:07 Main peak warm texture
- tool: palette knife.
- load: shadow mix + more brown + yellow ochre; deliberately not over-mixed.
- motion: press and drag/dab the knife edge down the right slope from the ridge toward the base, repeated, leaving broken mottled texture.
- placement: right face of the main peak from the top (~x 0.4, y 0.36) down to the lower right; also the right peak's right face.
- result: speckled orange-rust face (08:35, 08:51).
- rule: don't over-mix; the colors come out right on their own [08:23]
- seen: 08:35, 08:51, 09:07

### 09:27 Dark accents and light flecks
- tool: small palette knife.
- load: very dark mix (black/brown/blue).
- motion: slips into small places; vertical stroke along the left side of the main ridge; drag down; graze flat over dark so bits of light show through; slope out.
- placement: left face of the main peak and the ridge to its left (09:22-09:54).
- result: white flecks on the left face from 10:10; dry brush softens after (10:31). Check that the brushes are dry [10:17]
- seen: 09:22, 09:38, 09:54, 10:10, 10:31

### 10:33 Warm light on the main peak
- tool: palette knife.
- load: yellow ochre + bright red + sienna + white, not over-mixed (10:47).
- motion: lay and pull down from the ridge along the slope; bring it out toward the lower left to widen the base (11:04); then lighter version (more white) dabbed on top to sparkle.
- placement: whole right face, base spreading left and right.
- result: rust/orange face with pink dabs (11:04, 11:53, 12:10).
- seen: 10:47, 11:04, 11:37, 11:53, 12:10

### 11:44 Ledges and angle changes
- tool: small knife.
- load: shadow color.
- motion: dark shape behind a lit plane; changes the face angle; short flat dabs.
- placement: lower slope under the main peak.
- rule: the angles matter a great deal when painting mountains [12:04]

### 12:13 Grass slope
- tool: 2-in brush, bristles tapped in tiny cad yellow.
- motion: tapped and pulled wedge from the right foot of the mountain, blended sideways; touch of sap green here and there to separate; dark mix added at the lower left (12:57) and brought together; excess removed; softened with a clean brush as mist (13:26).
- placement: foot of the mountain, running down to the right into the water haze; dark band at left base.
- result: yellow-green grassy lower slope (12:43, 13:26, 13:40).
- seen: 12:29, 12:43, 12:57, 13:12, 13:26, 13:40

### 13:53 Far background trees
- tool: round brush (?), then dry brush.
- load: black + blue, + white, + brown; blue-gray.
- motion: vertical taps and short upward strokes making pointed conifers of varied height on a flat base line; several rows; dry-brush tap then light lift upward to soften.
- placement: below the mountain base, x ~0.15-0.55, y 0.50-0.60.
- result: blue silhouette treeline with pulled-down reflections (14:32, 14:50).
- seen: 14:13, 14:22, 14:32, 14:41, 14:50

### 14:56 Dark tree clumps and highlights
- tool: round brush.
- load: black + sap green + brown, darker than the far trees; then yellow ochre for highlights.
- motion: tapped loosely, clumps; few yellow-green taps on the upper right of the clumps, very light.
- placement: in front of the far trees at the shoreline, x ~0.2-0.7, y 0.6-0.7.
- result: dark island with yellow-green bushes (15:25, 15:51, 16:04).
- seen: 15:12, 15:25, 15:38, 15:51, 16:04

### 15:56 Reflections and waterline
- tool: 2-in brush pulled down; small knife.
- motion: grab paint at the base and pull straight down lightly; then horizontal across; knife with liquid white + blue (thin) cuts across for a water line; knife point knocks in sticks and twigs.
- placement: under the island, ~y 0.7-0.8.
- seen: 16:04, 16:17, 16:30, 16:35

### 16:45 Large dark trees at the sides
- tool: round brush.
- load: black, brown, green; dark sienna added.
- motion: tapped masses with pointed top, ragged lower edge dropping to the water.
- placement: big mass at the right middle (x ~0.7-0.95, y 0.25-0.65), smaller at the left edge.
- seen: 17:02, 17:15, 17:29, 17:42, 17:56
- rule: dark trees at the edges give borders and push everything back [17:47]

### 18:05 Trunks
- tool: fan brush.
- load: a little brown.
- motion: thin vertical indications, here and there.
- seen: 18:10

### 18:35 Highlights on trees and bushes
- tool: 1-in brush dipped in liquid white.
- load: yellows, greens, yellow ochre mixed on the brush; then bright red for an orange bush.
- motion: tapped on top of the clumps in layers; reflections: pull down, then brush sideways to bend them out.
- result: yellow-green and orange-red bushes (19:07, 19:21, 19:35).
- seen: 18:52, 19:07, 19:21, 19:35

### 19:45 Stones
- tool: palette knife, cleaned.
- load: Van Dyke brown.
- motion: edge set down making small flat stone blocks; a little pulled down for reflections; scattered in the water and along the shore; later brown + white on top edges.
- placement: along the shoreline from left-centre to centre-right, ~y 0.78, extending into the water.
- result: dark flat stones with pale tops (20:05, 20:55, 21:05).
- seen: 20:05, 20:15, 20:25, 20:55, 21:05

### 21:15 Cabin
- tool: palette knife.
- motion: lay a dark box with a slanted roof line; brown + white highlights on the front; knife cuts through the paint for slabs; brown door with white around it; red + brown on the roof laid from the knife; light color along the roof edge. Yellow bushes at the feet with a brush.
- placement: lower left, ~x 0.12-0.22, y 0.72-0.82, at the shoreline.
- result: weathered cabin with a red roof (22:07, 26:55).
- rule: layering creates depth in the painting [22:46]
- seen: 21:21, 21:32, 21:44, 21:55, 22:07

### 22:50 Large trees, limbs, leaves
- tool: brush for trunks (Van Dyke + sienna); liner brush with thinner + liquid black for limbs; liner with liquid white highlights; 2-in brush with cad yellow + sap green for leaves.
- motion: trunks tall and straight at both sides; a few thin bare limbs; highlights barely touch the canvas, then get a small pull; leaves tapped on loosely with a lot of paint, airy, shaped.
- placement: trunks at the left edge and right ~15% of the canvas; leaf clumps on the upper right tree and the mid-left thin tree.
- seen: 22:47, 23:11, 23:35, 24:00, 24:35, 25:35, 26:05, 26:15

## Rules and principles he states
- [00:39] leave holes in the sky.
- [01:47] over-blending kills the sky.
- [02:54] keep the cloud brush moving.
- [05:28] mountain: basic shape first, no detail yet.
- [06:00] pull down to remove excess paint so the next layer sticks.
- [07:03] farthest peak gets the tiniest amount of paint.
- [08:23] do not over-mix, colors happen automatically.
- [10:17] brushes must be dry when touching wet paint.
- [12:04] angles are very important in mountains.
- [15:20] darker color for nearer trees so they stand out; highlights show as trees get closer.
- [16:30] water lines help the illusion of depth.
- [17:47] dark trees at the edges frame the painting and push everything back.
- [25:28] leaves: a lot of paint, loose and airy.

## Finished painting
- focal point: main peak at about (0.38, 0.35), warm orange face against pale blue sky and white clouds.
- planes: far = sky, clouds, blue far hill (right); middle = main mountain, grass slope, blue conifer line, bushes; near = water, stones, cabin (lower left), tall trees at both edges.
- darkest darks: black on the left face of the main ridge and the dark trees at the edges; lightest: cloud tops and white flecks on the peak.
- framing: tall trunks at left and right with yellow leaf clumps.
- proportions: sky ~45%, mountains ~15%, trees/shore ~15%, water ~25% (?).
- temperature: cool blues in sky and water, warm orange mountain and bushes.
- note: the public reference image of the finished painting is a crop of the lower middle (cabin and water), not the whole painting; the whole painting is visible at video 27:04.

## Surprises
- The mountain is built almost entirely with palette knives; the orange face is a deliberately unmixed knife texture.
- Water is painted before the clouds, teal pulled from the outside in, with a gap left for the sheen.
- Mist at the mountain base comes from a horizontal brush pass over the pulled-down mountain paint.
