# S21E11 Desert Glow
- palette: Indian yellow, yellow ochre, alizarin crimson, phthalo blue, sap green, bright red, titanium white, midnight black, liquid white. Brown = sap green + alizarin crimson about 1:1, mixed thoroughly (not left marbled) [10:24-10:51]; it is the only ground/rock colour, lightened or darkened by amount of paint.
- canvas: 18x24 portrait. Contact paper with an OVAL cut out is stuck on; the whole canvas, oval included, is covered with a thin even coat of liquid white [01:07-01:25]. Oval (read from 07:50 and 26:25 frames) about x 0.12-0.85, y 0.09-0.90 of the canvas, slightly left of centre (?). The paper stays on until 22:19; the cactus is added outside the oval on the bare canvas.
- light: low sun glowing behind the horizon, which sits just below the middle of the oval (stated [03:18]); the glow is a yellow band with gold above it and crimson above that. Rock and butte are backlit silhouettes, with lighter red-brown planes on the left/top faces of the rock (?). No sun disc, no water.
- non-painting: 00:00-01:00 a live bird segment (no painting).

## Steps
### 01:41 Sky yellow: Indian yellow
- tool: 2-inch brush, flat on the canvas (as in the other ovals; angle not discussed) (?).
- load: very little Indian yellow, dry brush over the wet liquid white.
- motion: small criss-cross X strokes, light, bouncing the colour on.
- placement: a band in the middle of the oval, roughly y 0.38-0.60 of the oval, wider than tall, fading at its edges (frame 02:10); it is centred on the future horizon.
- result: soft yellow lozenge with a darker golden fringe appearing at the top (02:10).
- seen: 01:50, 02:10

### 02:01 Gold and crimson above the yellow
- tool: same brush, NOT cleaned between colours [02:01, 02:25].
- load: yellow ochre (a golden colour) straight onto the brush; then a small amount of alizarin crimson.
- motion: the same X strokes, each colour laid directly above the previous, going all the way to the top of the oval if wanted [02:50].
- placement: ochre directly above the yellow (y ~0.30-0.40), crimson above that up to the top of the oval (y ~0.10-0.30); the three colours stack as a bar of yellow, gold, orange-pink, with the lower half still white (frame 02:50, 03:10).
- result: a sunset egg: yellow low, orange mid, rose at the top, still with visible stroke direction.
- rule: blend until no one can see where a colour stops and the next starts [03:00].
- seen: 02:30, 02:50, 03:10

### 03:05 Lavender at the horizon
- tool: same 2-inch brush.
- load: crimson + a little phthalo blue = lavender [03:07-03:14].
- motion: lay the lavender and lift it in ONE direction, a pull curved upward at the end; then tap lightly to indicate thin stringy clouds along it [03:35-03:43]. The leftover paint is scrubbed off in the lower half of the oval, since it does not matter there [03:44].
- placement: the horizon is just below the oval's centre, y ~0.52-0.57; frame 03:34 shows a mauve streak running straight across at that height with its right end pointing slightly up. Scrub marks give a mauve cloud-shaped patch over y ~0.58-0.88 (frame 03:50), later covered by the ground colour.
- result: horizontal mauve line under the yellow glow; mauve patch below it.
- seen: 03:30, 03:50

### 03:58 Blend the sky
- tool: 2-inch brush washed in thinner, shaken out, dry.
- motion: start at the top and blend gently downward; the lower sky is only lightly blended to remove brush marks [04:00-04:12]. The pre-blend stringy horizon streak stays visible (frame 04:50).
- result: smooth colour fade from rose through gold to yellow, with the horizon line still marked.
- seen: 04:00, 04:12, 04:50

### 04:16 Big top cloud (fan brush)
- tool: #3 fan (any fan will do), flat to canvas.
- load: a large pile of lavender made with much more crimson than blue [04:22-04:26]; knife cleaned first.
- motion: a stroke that is a very exaggerated slant at first, then smaller; the fan strokes are laid overlapping each other like waves of an ocean [05:08-05:14]; the ends of the bristles trail to one side so the cloud has a feathery edge (frame 05:34, 06:14).
- placement: at the top of the oval, y ~0.03-0.22, left to right x ~0.15-0.80; its lower right is a stepped, ragged edge that runs down toward the right (frame 06:38).
- result: a big dark mauve feathered cloud cap at the top of the oval over the rose sky.
- seen: 05:02, 05:34, 06:14, 06:38

### 05:20 Small soft cloud and blending

### 07:58 Butte (far mesa)
- tool: corner of the fan brush; then a 2-inch brush.
- load: the same lavender, a touch more blue, i.e. the blue side of lavender [08:01-08:04]; thick enough to be a solid silhouette.
- motion: fan corner presses the paint on and pulls it DOWNWARD in short strokes; each pull makes a vertical rock face, so the top is jagged with several little pillars (frames 08:28, 08:51, 09:14). Then a thin flat line is pulled out to each side as the horizon foot. The bottom is then pulled out with the 2-inch, leaving a haze under it so it joins the ground [09:24-09:34].
- placement: left of centre, butte x ~0.22-0.40 of the oval, summit y ~0.52-0.56, base line y ~0.60; foot lines run across about x 0.12-0.45. Small and far, no detail [09:18].
- result: a purple mesa with 4-5 spires and a long flat base on the horizon, softly merging with the haze (frames 09:37, 10:00).
- rule: it is far away, so little detail [09:18].
- seen: 08:28, 08:51, 09:14, 09:37, 10:00

### 10:21 Brown mix
- tool: knife on the palette.
- load: sap green and alizarin crimson, about equal, mixed hard until even; this gives one of the best browns [10:24-10:51]. The surface of the mixed pile is pale brown, with dark flecks (frame 10:51).

### 10:59 Desert ground
- tool: 2-inch brush.
- load: a little of the brown on the brush.
- motion: begin very lightly just below the horizon and add more paint toward the bottom; things get darker and darker in value as they come toward the viewer [11:10-11:26]; more paint = darker [11:32].
- placement: fills the lower oval from the horizon (y ~0.58) to the bottom; paint stops short of the oval edge, so a mauve fringe is visible (frame 11:42). The result is lighter in the middle and darker towards the lower left, right and bottom edges, a vignette (frames 12:08, 19:35).
- result: a light-to-dark brown ground; the value gap alone gives distance [11:48-11:53].
- rule: value change from light at the horizon to dark in front creates the feeling of distance [11:45-12:00].
- seen: 11:20, 11:42, 12:08

### 13:42 Rock colour and texture
- tool: knife.
- load: bright red + a little of the brown + a little white; desert colours are vivid, so the palette can be wild [13:43-14:05]. Knife wiped first; small roll of paint cut off.
- motion: ZERO pressure, only a graze over the canvas, like painting a mountain [14:09-14:19]; a mountain is treated as a big rock [14:14]. The knife can be turned during the stroke [14:28]. The paint breaks, so holes and openings are left; they are the point [14:42-14:47]. Strokes run down the rock face, steeply, and are rough and ragged [15:06]. A dark area is left as a recess [15:15].
- placement: on the whole rock except a dark hollow lower on the face and the dark base; lighter red planes gather on the left and top faces (frames 14:31, 15:25), the right side stays dark (?).
- result: textured red-brown rock with cracks, light/dark planes (frames 14:58, 15:25).
- rule: keep the holes in the paint [14:43-14:47]; leave a dark area so a recess is read [15:15].
- seen: 14:04, 14:31, 14:58, 15:25

## Rules and principles he states
- Colours go on without cleaning the brush: yellow, gold, crimson in that order, then blend until no colour edge is visible [02:01-03:03].
- Horizon just below the centre of the oval [03:18].
- Pull lavender in one direction and tap for stringy clouds [03:24-03:40].
- Lavender = crimson + blue; for the clouds, much more crimson than blue [04:22-04:26]; a bluer lavender for mountains [08:01].
- Blend the bottom of a far cloud out so it stays quiet, then a cloud in front reads as nearer [05:44-06:11].
- Far mountain: little detail [09:18-09:22].
- Mix sap green + crimson thoroughly for brown, unlike other mixes, which are left marbled [10:43-10:49].
- Ground and rocks get darker and darker as they come forward; more paint makes it darker; this value gap makes distance [11:14-11:56].
- Rock colours can be wild in the desert: red, brown, white [13:43-14:05].
- Knife rock texture: no pressure at all, graze, let the paint break and keep the holes [14:09-14:47].
- A mountain is treated like a big rock [14:14]; a dark recess is left to hide critters [15:15].
- Pull a flat ledge from the top only, then drag down for a sharp edge; it pushes the earlier stone back [15:51-16:20].
- A path blends back by pulling in ONE direction, then a very light cross pass [19:06-19:20].
- Double-load a brush (light on one side, dark on the other) for little stones [22:02-22:11].
- No square cactus, give it round tops and arms [23:16]; keep it a silhouette with only a little green [25:15].
- A subject outside the frame needs a base so it does not hang [23:46-23:49].

- Darkest darks: the rock's hollows and base shadow, the cactus trunk, the weeds at the cactus foot. Lightest lights: the yellow glow at the horizon, and the light dirt in the middle of the ground.
- Proportions inside the oval (by height): sky ~0.55 (horizon at about 0.55-0.60), ground ~0.40; the rock is as high as 0.37 of the oval; a ground that is always lighter in the centre and darker at the oval rim.
- Light: from behind at the horizon; the glow is centred; warm yellow-rose sky, purple clouds and butte (cool accents), brown-red warm ground and rock. Overall a warm earth palette with lavender as the only cool colour.

## Surprises
- The sky is yellow first (not pink or blue): yellow, gold and crimson stacked by one uncleaned brush with the same X stroke, and the lavender is only for clouds and the far mesa.
- A fan brush makes the clouds with overlapping slanted strokes, then a dry 2-inch blends the bottom out (back cloud) while the front cloud is kept sharper.
- The butte uses only the corner of the fan, pulled downward, not a knife.
- Sap green + crimson makes the brown for everything (ground, rock, cactus); the ground is a light-to-dark value gradient, not a series of colours.
- Rocks and the big boulder are done with the knife at zero pressure, as for a mountain, with bright red, brown and white used for rock colour.
- The path is made by blending light dirt back in one direction, not by painting a line.
- Little stones are a single touch with a double-loaded filbert (light/dark).
- The oval is peeled late and the cactus is put outside the oval on bare canvas, with its foot mound and grass making a frame-break (the same device as the tree in other ovals).
- Gaps: the tool for the path at 18:22 is not clear; the side of the cactus where the ribs are lit is not clear; the oval fractions are read from low-resolution frames and are rough; the butte and rock positions in oval fractions are estimates from the final frames.
