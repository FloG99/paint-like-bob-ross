# Color

## How he thinks about color
- **Value before hue.** What matters is dark against light. Darks frame the light, and most of
  a painting is mid-to-dark with a few bright lights. [S11E01 18:34, S12E10 12:36]
- **Mix on the canvas, not the palette.** Colors are tapped onto a wet base, layered one over
  another with the same unwashed brush, and half-mixed on the brush or knife. Colors are left
  marbled on purpose. [S07E04 08:23, S17E07 14:31]
- **Tiny amounts.** Sky colors use the smallest possible amount. The strong tubes (phthalo blue,
  Prussian blue) are a touch. Alizarin is weak, so it is laid first and blue goes on over it
  with no wash in between. [S12E13 1:31, S20E09 02:31]
- **One color family per painting.** The dark mix of the mountain becomes the trees, banks,
  rocks and bark. Sky colors go into the water. That repetition is the harmony. [S12E10 21:14]
- **Temperature split.** The lit sides are warm: white + red, ochre or sienna. The shadow sides are cool: white + blue. The sky and water are cool, and the foliage is warm accents. [S12E10 09:27, S07E04]
- **Distance = air.** Far things are lighter, bluer or more lavender, and lower in contrast. Each plane forward is darker and warmer. [S08E06 12:16, S07E04 15:20]

## Over all 403 episodes
Seven tubes appear in more than 80% of all paintings: white, alizarin, Van Dyke, cadmium yellow, ochre, phthalo blue and bright red. How the palette shifts by subject:
- **Mountains:** Prussian blue (81% of these paintings vs 65% of all).
- **Rivers:** more Indian yellow, sap green and ochre.
- **Winter:** drops sap green (20% vs 76%) and the yellows.
- **Night:** uses black gesso (82%). In both night studies the palette is almost only white plus one dark; the color is in the ground, and the scene stays cool except for the cabin. [S08E13 01:29, S01E06 00:07]
