# Materials, tools, loading

## Paints
His fixed palette: about a dozen tubes, a painting uses 8–13 (median 11 over all 403
episodes, `dataset/stats.md`). Roles as used in the studied episodes:

| tube | role |
|---|---|
| Titanium White | everything light: snow, clouds, highlights, water lines (thinned) |
| Phthalo Blue | skies, water, snow shadows; very strong, use a touch |
| Prussian Blue | the darks (trees, mountains), snow shadows; used in a strong, concentrated mix |
| Midnight Black | darks; transparent over glow; with blue = sky and water |
| Alizarin Crimson | sky warmth, lavender (with blue), purple mountains, winter warm tint |
| Van Dyke Brown | earth, banks, rocks, trunks, part of every dark mix |
| Dark Sienna | warm earth, trunks (with white), warm mountain light |
| Sap Green | greens, mixed into tree darks; never in winter |
| Cadmium Yellow | yellow-greens for lit grass and leaves (with sap green) |
| Yellow Ochre | warmer, duller highlights; autumn |
| Indian Yellow | warm glow, rich lit greens and autumn |
| Bright Red | a touch in white (cloud tops, glowing snow), autumn sparkle |
| Phthalo Green | occasional: water (with blue), evergreen darks (with Prussian) |

**Mediums**
- **Liquid White**: the usual base coat, thin and even. It also thins paint for layers that must stick on top, and it is the paint for water lines.
- **Liquid Clear**: base coat for transparent glowing skies (black stays black on it). Also thins white for waterfalls. Use very little. [S12E10 00:06, S17E07 11:44]
- **Liquid Black**: a dark base, or thinned for fine limbs.
- **Odorless thinner**: brush wash, and thinning liner paint.

## Base coats other than Liquid White
Over all 403 paintings: Black Gesso in 114 (28%), Liquid Clear in 51 (13%), Liquid Black in 19 (5%); night paintings use Black Gesso in 82% (`dataset/stats.md`). Frame-checked in four studied episodes:
- **Liquid Clear over the whole canvas** (S11E03): the tiniest amount, a very thin coat. Colors laid on it stay transparent and give a softer look than on white; where it dries it turns glossy like a glaze. Use very little color too. It also thins white for flowers and brown for grass. [S11E03 00:55, 01:40, 23:55]
  - **Spatter**: dip a fan in thinner and flick drops off it with a knife edge onto wet color on Clear, a few at a time; they open into pale round cells that read as masses of far leaves and keep growing as they sit (thinner spatter or blotches named in 8 episodes). [S11E03 05:00, 05:10, S27E05 07:52, S30E05 06:56]
- **Split coat** (S10E10): Liquid White on the sky, Liquid Clear on the sea, so the sea's dark keeps its value instead of picking up white. [S10E10 00:20, 09:35]
- **Black ground with transparent color** (S08E13): flat black acrylic, dried, then thick transparent color (alizarin, phthalo green, phthalo blue) laid wet over all of it. Only white is added for the lights: it picks up the color beneath. The darks need very little paint, since the ground is dark. [S08E13 00:11, 01:29, 04:44]
- **Black ground with a thin dark wash** (S01E06): a thin transparent coat of Van Dyke + Prussian blue that still reads black; the glow, moon, snow and frost are all white added onto it, and the black is left to show as night. [S01E06 00:07, 16:58]
- **Rules on a dark ground**:
  - Use transparent colors for color; they let the black show through (transparent named with a dark or black base in 14 episodes). [S08E13 19:44, S01E06 16:58, S02E03 00:00, S16E11 00:01]
  - Test a color by rubbing a little on the black: if it vanishes it is transparent; if it stays visible (bright red) it is opaque and will not work there. [S08E13 19:29]
  - Blend with a dry brush; a wet brush cuts through to the black. [S19E12 03:38]

## Tools and the part that touches
| tool | part used | for |
|---|---|---|
| 2" brush | flat face | sky criss-cross, water pulls, blending, reflections, big dark masses, grass taps |
| 2" brush | top corner | cumulus blend, mist taps, bushes |
| 1" brush | flat face / corner / chisel | clouds (small circles), evergreens (corner zigzag), bushes and leaves (push up) |
| fan brush (#3 small, #6 big) | corner / edge / flat | evergreens, distant tree lines, waterfalls and foam, ripples, trunks |
| large palette knife | long edge with a roll of paint | mountain outline, snow, shadows, rocks, banks, water lines |
| small knife | long edge, tip | tight snow spots, shadows, rock lights, cabins |
| knife, clean edge or tip | scraping | trunks and twigs cut through wet paint, scraped highlights |
| liner (script) brush | tip, thinned paint | limbs, twigs, signature |

What this adds to the text:
- **Blending and big masses are the 2"**: sky, cloud, mist, water and reflection. For mountains the 2" does the base and pull-down and the knife the outline and snow (82% and 69%, both in most mountains).
- **The fan** is the evergreen brush (83%) and the waterfall and ocean brush; it is also the commonest grass brush (48%).
- **Bushes** are split evenly between the 2" corner and the 1" (42% vs 40%); leaf trees between the 2" and the fan (42% each).
- **Trunks**: the liner is the commonest tool (65%), the knife second (54%), the fan third (35%).
- **Water lines** are a knife job (72%) and Liquid White the usual paint (43%), more often than plain white.
- **Structures** are almost pure knife (91%), with the small knife for door, window and chimney (36%); the liner is the tool in 8%.
- **Mountain colour**: crimson is in 74% of mountains, not just purple ones, as a warm tint in the mix.

## Loading (how paint gets on the tool)
- **Tap in**: push the bristles straight down into the paint repeatedly, so color goes evenly through them. For sky colors; without it, big blue streaks. [S20E09 02:43]
- **Pull through one direction**: drag the brush through the pile so the paint rides on one side. For bushes and leaves (a lot of color). [S19E13 25:40]
- **Two colors on one brush**: tap one color, then just touch another. The strokes then come out mixed and varied (many different things on the brush at once). [S19E13 14:39]
- **Chisel edge**: pull the 1" brush through the pile while wiggling it, so it ends in a sharp edge. Reshape it between trees. [S12E02 16:56]
- **Fan, both sides full**: for evergreens and tree lines, loaded very full.
- **Knife roll**:
  1. Spread the paint very flat on the palette.
  2. Cut straight across it with the long edge.
  3. A thin roll of paint now sits on the edge, and only that roll touches the canvas.

  For snow, use firm, dry paint. Several colors laid side by side and cut together give a streaky multi-color roll. [S11E01 07:53, S12E10 08:09]
- **Dip first**: touch Liquid White, Liquid Clear or thinner, then the color, so the stroke flows and sticks over thick paint. For waterfalls, water lines, snow bushes and highlights.

## Cleaning
Scrub the brush in odorless thinner, shake it, beat it against the can, and wipe it. Do this
before every blend, mist pass and color change. Wipe the knife often on a paper towel.
Blending brushes must be **dry**. [S11E01 03:28, S12E02 3:08]

## Pressure scale used in these cards
| word | 0–1 | what it means |
|---|---|---|
| whisper | 0.05–0.15 | barely touching the surface, paint breaks, holes left |
| light | 0.2–0.35 | blending, reflections, mist taps |
| medium | 0.4–0.6 | normal painting strokes |
| firm | 0.65–0.85 | grass and bank taps, pushes that bend the bristles |
| hard | 0.9–1 | mountain outline pressed firmly into the canvas, scraping |
