# S08E13 Northern Lights
- palette: titanium white, midnight black, phthalo blue, Van Dyke brown, dark sienna, Liquid White (to thin the white). Bright red only for a test swatch. Already on the canvas before the video (transparent colors): alizarin crimson, phthalo green, phthalo blue (+ green mixed).
- canvas: pre-painted. Flat black acrylic, left to dry, then thick transparent color laid over all of it: crimson on one side, phthalo green and phthalo blue mixed elsewhere, so the whole surface is wet and covered (00:11-00:40). No Liquid White coat. Portrait-leaning, nearly square format (?).
- light: night. Two sources: the aurora in the upper half (cool blue-white, lavender where the crimson lies) and, at the end, a faint cool glow on snow everywhere (mountain, bushes, roof). No moon, no stars. The only warm color is the cabin wood (brown). No lit windows.
- positions are fractions of the canvas, x from the left edge, y from the top. Read mostly from the finished frame (25:52) and from zoomed frames, so approximate (?).
- the paint on the canvas is already wet: white laid over it picks up the color below. The ground color does the coloring, the tubes on the palette are mostly white and dark blue-black.

## Steps

### 01:32 Aurora: tap in three curtains
- tool: fan brush, flat to the canvas, tapping (not dragging).
- load: titanium white only, loaded full. The color comes from the ground [01:29].
- motion: little taps along a curved path, building a thin stippled band; more paint and more taps where the band should be brighter; the line wavers (small ridges that bounce) rather than being smooth. Each band is a single sweeping curve, about 0.02 wide.
- placement (? zoomed frames, canvas top edge visible): band A from the left edge (x 0, y ~0.2) arcing right and down toward (0.55, 0.5); band B from the top (x ~0.2, y 0) curving down to about (0.6, 0.5); band C, the biggest, from the top right (x ~0.7, y 0), out to the right edge (x ~0.9, y 0.25) and hooking back down-left to about (0.5, 0.5). The bands are roughly parallel, nested like layers (three layers here; any number is fine).
- result: three white dotted arcs, dense in the lower part of the curve.
- rule: curtains or layers, draw only basic shapes first [01:03, 01:44].
- seen: 01:35, 01:46, 01:57, 02:08, 02:20, 02:22

### 02:22 Aurora: rays with the 1 in brush
- tool: 1 in brush, flat, hair pressed slightly.
- load: none added; it takes the white from the arcs and the ground color beneath.
- motion: set the brush on the band and flick it upward (away from the curve's center, upward on the canvas) in a short pop, repeat all along the arc. Rays follow the local direction of the curve, so on the left band they stand almost vertical, on the right band they lean with the hook. Each flick starts at the bright edge and fades out; the rays show vertical streaks 0.1-0.25 high.
- placement: above the lower edge of each band. The lower edge stays hard and bright, the rays fade above it. (? the hard line is the arc itself.)
- result: soft blue-white streaks, lavender where the crimson is, deeper blue elsewhere. The old white arcs now read as the bright base line of each curtain.
- rule: the lights take their color from what is underneath [03:50-04:03].
- seen: 02:34, 02:47, 03:00

### 03:11 Aurora: rays with the 2 in brush
- tool: 2 in brush, flat.
- motion: go through the entire set of bands with long upward lifts, same angles as before; pull as high as wanted (some rays reach the canvas top on the left, y 0). Upward lifts only, no scrubbing back and forth.
- placement: all three curtains, the dark centre of the curve (the hook interior, x 0.3-0.5, y 0.3-0.45) is left dark.
- result: a clear spiral of blue and white light, hook-shaped, darkest in the middle; the strongest white at the lower edges of the curve (x 0.5-0.65, y 0.45-0.5).
- rule: pull as high as you like; less colorful regions can use only white or yellow [03:25-03:37] (colors here: blue, lavender, a little cyan-green (?)).
- seen: 03:13, 03:26, 03:39, 03:52, 04:05, 04:15

### 04:18 Mountain: dark silhouette with the knife
- tool: palette knife, cut across a thin spread on the palette so a roll sits on the edge.
- load: black + phthalo blue + a little Van Dyke, dark. Very little paint, because the canvas is already dark [04:44].
- motion: short stabs and slides, bumps, hills and valleys along the outline; then scrape off every excess firmly (it can hardly be scraped too hard).
- placement: left of centre. Peak about x 0.28, y 0.27; the ridge runs down right to a tail at about x 0.8, y 0.55; base near y 0.65-0.7.
- result: a nearly invisible dark shape on the dark blue sky (? only the outline matters here).
- rule: choose where the mountain lives; the second layer sticks easier the less paint is left [05:13-05:19].
- seen: 04:31, 04:43, 04:55

### 05:07 Mountain: pull with the large brush
- tool: large flat brush (2 in (?)), clean.
- motion: grab the edge paint and pull it downward, along the slopes; lighter pulls shape where highlights and shadows might go; can be redone any number of times.
- result: grey streaked mountain with brush marks; excess paint gone.
- rule: this is also the way to try out highlights and shadows without commitment [05:26-05:45].
- seen: 05:07, 05:19, 05:31, 05:43

### 05:55 Mountain: snow with the knife
- tool: palette knife, flat spread, then cut for a small roll.
- load: titanium white + small blue to tint (pale blue-white). Firm paint [08:33].
- motion: no pressure at all; let the paint flow down the slope so it breaks and leaves small holes; a wiggle of the knife adds texture. Start at the top of the peak and pull down along each ridge. Where the snow joins, let it join.
- placement: first stroke along the right ridge (x 0.45-0.8, y 0.4-0.55), then the main peak top (x 0.28, y 0.27) with strokes down both slopes, then a second ridge to the right (peak about x 0.49, y 0.41).
- result: ragged white on the dark ridges, grainy, dark showing through.
- rule: decisions made as it goes, no plan needed [07:00]; snow follows the angle of the slopes [25:44].
- seen: 06:10, 06:18, 06:27, 06:36, 06:45, 06:53, 07:02, 07:11

### 07:17 Mountain: shadows and extra peaks
- tool: palette knife.
- load: blue + white, lightened a second time because the first was too dark; same roll on the edge.
- motion: zero pressure, barely brush the canvas, short strokes at the slope angles; drop in many small ridges. Pull one stroke through to push a form back. Play light and shadow back and forth, as in the daylight episodes. A small peak gets its own blue shadow beside it, so it reads as a separate form [08:44-09:20].
- placement: small peak at the left (x 0.15, y 0.37), the dark valley pocket on the lower left of the main mass (x 0.2-0.3, y 0.45-0.55), shadow streaks on the right faces (x 0.35-0.55, y 0.35-0.5), another small peak on the far right.
- result: faceted white and blue peaks with a deep dark pocket; snow lighter at the top, blue lower.
- rule: every peak needs its own shadow side to stand out [08:46-08:52]; the paint blends with the color below [09:25].
- seen: 07:20, 07:36, 07:52, 08:08, 08:25, 08:41, 08:57, 09:13, 09:30

### 09:31 Mountain: mist at the base
- tool: clean dry 2 in brush.
- motion: gentle taps following the angles; barely any pressure near the top (a whisper), harder taps at the base. Beat the brush against the can when it gets loaded, no full wash. Then lift the brush gently upward to erase tap marks and make mist.
- placement: lower third of the mountain, y 0.55-0.68, across the whole width.
- result: a soft blue-white haze at the foot; the snow fades into the dark.
- rule: follow angles; the mist stays as a separator for the trees [11:37-11:45].
- seen: 09:39, 09:48, 09:58, 10:07, 10:16, 10:26, 10:35

### 10:43 Far evergreens
- tool: fan brush, top part (corner) of the fan, tapping downward.
- load: black + phthalo blue + Van Dyke mixed on the brush (reused from the mountain mix), fan full.
- motion: small downward taps stacking up thin pointed trees; spacing close, or they look like posts. Keep the mist above them untouched.
- placement: along the mountain's foot, y 0.65-0.75, across x 0.05-0.85; tree tops reach into the mist only slightly.
- result: a row of thin, dark spiky trees at the base of the mountain; the mist glows behind them.
- rule: too few trees look like fence posts, add more [12:01-12:10]; keep the mist so trees do not merge into the mountain [11:39].
- seen: 11:01, 11:18, 11:35, 11:52, 12:09, 12:26, 12:43

### 12:24 Light at the tree bases

- rule: play light against dark and dark against light continuously [13:00]. seen: 13:00, 13:15

### 13:15 Dark bushes

## Rules and principles he states
- [01:03] Aurora comes as curtains or layers.
- [01:11] Without aurora in your area, leave the lights out and put a small moon in the sky.
- [01:29] White is enough because the color is already on the canvas.
- [01:49] More paint where it should be brighter.
- [03:25] Pull rays as high as you like; where you live the lights can be just white or yellow.
- [04:00] Colors under the white show through: lavender, blue, green-blue mixes.
- [04:44] The mountain needs very little paint because the ground is black.
- [05:13] Removing excess paint makes the next layer stick.
- [06:27] Zero pressure with the knife so the paint breaks.
- [08:33] Snow paint should be firm.
- [08:46] Each small peak needs its own shadow.
- [09:44] Follow the angles of the mountain; tap harder at the base than at the top.
- [11:39] Do not wipe out the mist, it separates mountain from trees.
- [12:01] Too few small trees look like posts.
- [13:00] Light against dark and dark against light, continuously.
- [15:15] Reflection: start at the brightest spot, work outward, it darkens away from it.
- [17:12] Too many highlights on far trees: cover them with the dark paint.
- [19:01] The light source changes a black-ground painting a lot.
- [19:29] Transparency test: a transparent color rubs out to black, an opaque color stays.
- [24:04] Keep dark contrast, do not highlight everything.
- [26:13] Work in layers, most distant first.

## Surprises
- The ground is pre-colored: tubes of transparent color were laid on black acrylic beforehand, so the sky's colors are made by moving white across, not by painting color. Only white is added for the lights.
- The aurora comes from white arcs tapped with the fan brush and then flicked upward into rays with the 1 in and 2 in brush; the rays are a lift, not a painted stroke. The tapped arc remains as the bright lower edge.
- The mountain uses very little paint, and the form is found with a brush pull first, then snow and shadow with the knife (the same knife-point method as day paintings, with firmer paint and zero pressure).
- A cool, almost monochrome night: warm color appears only on the cabin, with no lit window. Snow is blue-white throughout, and the glow around the bushes comes from the ground color showing through.
- Painted in the usual back to front order: sky, mountain, mist, far trees, shore, water, big trees, bushes, cabin, foreground snow. Big dark trees are placed after the lake and cross the aurora.
- Snow-covered bushes are made with one pull-through of white in one direction and a single touch-and-push, no scrubbing.
