## Steps
### 01:09 Sky pink: alizarin crimson, criss-cross
- tool: 2-inch brush, flat, bristles to canvas at moderate angle (~45-60° (?)).
- load: a small amount of alizarin crimson on the dry brush over the liquid white base. Brush NOT cleaned afterward, since alizarin crimson is not especially potent [02:31].
- motion: small criss-cross X strokes, ~2-3 in each (?), light pressure, bouncing the colour on. Work upward from the middle, adding until the pink reaches near the oval top (about this far, he indicates [02:14]).
- placement: thickest in the middle band; it looks like a large painted spring egg [02:20]. At 02:14 pink covers about y 0.12-0.80 of the oval; the lowest part stays white (water zone) and the top edge fades out.
- result: soft, even pink egg, darker at center, no hard edges inside the oval.
- seen: 01:11, 01:27, 01:42, 01:58, 02:14, 02:30

### 02:33 Sky blue at top and water blue at bottom
- tool: same uncleaned 2-inch brush.
- load: touch of phthalo blue; push the bristles into the blue and tap them so the colour distributes evenly through them; otherwise large blue streaks form that are difficult to remove [02:43].
- motion: start at the top of the oval, short side-to-side strokes, laying a band of blue along the top inside the oval edge, then allow it to soften downward [03:02]. He mentions tapping in stringy clouds [03:09] (not done).
- placement: blue arc along the top ~0.00-0.18 of oval height with pink below. While blue is still on the brush, two blue patches low in the oval (water): one low left, one low right, y ~0.70-0.85 (frames 03:26, 03:43). Not a full band; pink/white remains between them.
- result: strong cyan-blue arc at the top, two blue lozenges at the bottom, pink in between.
- seen: 02:35, 02:52, 03:09, 03:26, 03:43, 04:00

### 03:59 Blend the sky

- load: nothing.
- motion: horizontal sweeps across the oval, extremely gentle, all the way across, almost no pressure, just enough to merge; start at the top blue and work down through the pink; same across the bottom blue patches. Blue meets pink = a lavender-type tone [04:19].
- result: smooth gradient cyan -> lavender -> pink -> lavender/blue at the bottom; brush marks gone (04:17, 04:35).
- rule: little pressure, only what is needed to merge the tones [04:31]
- seen: 04:17, 04:35

## Rules and principles he states
- Limited palette, few colors [01:04]; errors are treated as chances to change the plan [14:03].
- Warm winter: pink in the sky [03:34]; cold winter: omit pink, use blue/gray/midnight black [03:47].
- Load a brush with blue by tapping into the bristles so it spreads evenly; avoids streaks [02:43].
- Pink then blue with the same brush, no cleaning, because alizarin is weak [02:31].
- Clean dry 2-inch brush to blend the sky, very light pressure [04:07-04:31].
- Clouds: light first or dark first, blend gently [06:19]; shade the base, light comes from above [06:34]; fluff with the corner of a dry brush; clouds in front of clouds [06:54].
- Light from the right, easier for right-handers [09:52]; snow goes where the light hits [10:09].
- Mountain shadow knife strokes: no pressure, keep the holes [11:20-11:31]; don't overdo dark crevices [12:32].
- Follow the angles of the strokes when blending [12:55, 13:15].
- A little hill gives another plane and pushes things back [13:42].
- Tree variety: crooked, with character; limbs up or down depending on the species [15:19, 15:35].
- Dark tree mix is near-black, Prussian blue gives the blue cast [17:41-17:52].
- Thin paint sticks to thick paint; liquid white thins colors for the bush snow [21:16-21:21].
- Work in layers, one layer at a time [21:33].
- Reflections: touch lightly, pull straight down, then go across [19:50-19:55, 21:45].
- Think about shape and form; farther trees get less detail [25:28, 25:55].
- A path needs bushes on both sides to sit in the scene [26:41].
- Wipe the knife often [17:11]; thinner for big brushes, paper towel for small ones [07:37].

## Surprises
- There is no snow field and no stream: the snow ground is only a lip of snow on the lake bank, a foreground snow path, and snow-capped bushes. The water is a still lake. Snow does not read as water because it sits on shore forms: bumpy slanted edges cut by dark bushes, versus the hard horizontal water line and vertical dark reflections.
- Mountain snow and shadows are laid with the knife at almost zero pressure, then tapped (not stroked) with a dry brush along the same diagonals.
- The picture is an oval: the contact paper is peeled off late and the big tree is added over the bare canvas, so the tree breaks the oval frame.
- The sky is pink first, blue on top, blended in one dry-brush pass; no white in the sky before the clouds.
- Evergreens use black + Prussian blue + crimson (not sienna), so they look blue-black and take blue-white highlights as if frosted.
- Liquid white is used only to thin pink-white so it sticks on thick dark paint for snow bushes.
- Gaps: the tool for the sienna trunk hints and for the small bush highlights is not clear in frames; the tool for the shadow tap that forms the small hill [13:32] is unclear; the knife stroke path for the dark mountain is inferred.
