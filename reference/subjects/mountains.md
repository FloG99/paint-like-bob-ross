# Mountains

> Knife a dark outline, scrape it, pull it down into mist. Float white down the lit slopes, then float a blue shadow at the opposite angle beside each highlight, so every highlight has its shadow. Tap the base into mist along the angles.

Counts (N/M): episodes whose narration names the step / episodes that paint the subject. They are lower bounds: not everything done is said.

## Steps
1. **Dark outline** (knife, hard):
   - Mix midnight black + Prussian blue + Van Dyke + a touch of alizarin; it should look black. Spread it flat and cut a roll onto the long edge. A dark knife mix with no white shows up in 119/192 (black 88, alizarin 93, Van Dyke 72).
   - Hold the blade nearly upright and drag the ridge from the peak down both sides, lifting and re-placing for bumps and steps. Make a stepped summit and a long tail (22/192).
   - Push the paint firmly into the canvas (46/192). Only the outer edge matters (32/192). Fill the inside roughly.
   - [S11E01 07:53, S12E02 6:00, S19E13 06:49]
2. **Scrape**: knife flat, firm, take off all the excess, so the next layer sticks (52/192). [S12E13 4:58, S12E02 06:40]
3. **Pull down**:
   - With the 2" flat brush, grab the paint at the ridge and pull straight down or down the slopes, lifting off toward the base (86/192).
   - Then add light horizontals at the base so it fades out completely.
   - The top stays crisp: the mountain is more distinct at the top than at the bottom, because of mist.
   - [S11E01 09:13, S19E13 07:33, S12E02 07:08]
4. **Snow on the lit faces** (knife float, whisper):
   - Use firm, dry titanium white (a white knife step in 92/192), adding a touch of bright red for warm light (24/192). Use a thin roll.
   - Set the knife on the ridge and let it slide down the slope at the slope's angle, 0.5–3" (angles stated in 42/192). Only the roll touches. Use no pressure at all (63/192), so the paint breaks and the dark shows through.
   - Lit faces only (light usually from the right). All highlight strokes run at the same angle. More at the peak, thinner lower down.
   - [S12E02 8:10, S12E13 6:14, S19E13 09:55, S10E12 11:07]
5. **Shadows** (opposing float):
   - Use white + a little Prussian or phthalo blue (+ black or brown to dull it, 55/192).
   - Same whisper touch, at the **opposite angle** (said outright in 18/192), laid on the shadow faces right beside each highlight, in valleys and under protrusions. Use a small knife in tight spots (28/192).
   - Every highlight needs its own shadow (a shadow step in 88/192). A peak only pops once it has its shadow.
   - [S12E02 9:20, S12E13 8:20, S19E13 12:42, S13E02 09:36]
6. **Overlap**: paint the back peak's shadow first, then let the front peak's highlight flow over it, which pushes that one back (47/192). Add small peaks anywhere: highlight first, then its shadow. [S12E13 8:48, 9:40, S07E04 06:55]
7. **Mist** (tap and lift):
   - With a clean dry 2", tap along the base following the slope angles, lifting on each tap (tap and lift in 99/183 mist episodes; following the angles in 25/183). Pull it together horizontally only in the middle.
   - Never sweep straight across: that gives a hard, sharp-edged cut.
   - [S11E01 12:54, S20E09 12:46, S12E10 11:27]
8. **More ranges**: each range forward is lower and darker, and the mist goes between them (85/192 describe layering or lowering). Add foothill humps with a fan and tiny highlights (fan, 1" or round for hills, 36/192). [S08E06 11:39, 14:15, S08E10 15:41]

## Variants across episodes
- **Warm light**:
  - Instead of white, lay dark sienna + bright red + yellow ochre + white on the knife unmixed, so the streaks change color by themselves (27/192).
  - Shadows are Prussian + white + a touch of sienna, barely mixed.
  - The face reads rust or orange against a blue shadow plane.
  - [S07E04 10:33, S12E10 08:09]
- **Far mountain**: use very little paint, push in one small peak, and blend it sideways so it appears to float. At most a token highlight, tapped soft. No detail (31/192 say small or detail-free). [S19E13 05:04, S07E04 03:48]
- **Lavender or purple range**: black + phthalo + more alizarin (30/192). Too much blue makes mountains green. [S08E06 05:28, S15E08 04:27]
- **Brush-built mountains (no knife)**: tap the dark shape in with the 2", then tap white into it in layers, darker where the face turns, for rolling hills and soft ranges (30/192). [S15E08 08:58, S08E06 08:18, S09E07 15:37]
- **Grassy foot**: tap a yellow-green wedge at the slope angle along the base (33/192). Lift straight up from its dark flecks to make hundreds of tiny trees. [S19E13 14:23, 16:07, S15E08 08:18]

## Proportions
- The summit sits at y 0.08–0.35 and x 0.3–0.5. The range spans about 0.5–0.8 of the width, with one side often running off the canvas.
- Snow covers most of the lit faces down to about two-thirds of the slope, then breaks into flecks.

## Mistakes
- Pressure on snow, which makes mud.
- Wet or thin white.
- Highlights at random angles, or shadows at the same angle as the highlights.
- No shadows.
- Detail near the base.
- Blending across the angles.
- A hard bottom edge.
