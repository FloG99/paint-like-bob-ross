# The method in one page

Wet-on-wet ("alla prima") oil landscape as taught on The Joy of Painting: one sitting, about
26 minutes, on a canvas pre-coated with a thin wet base. Every rule below is stated by him or
seen in every studied episode; references point to `studies/` as `SxxExx mm:ss`.

## The laws
1. **The canvas is wet.** A thin coat of Liquid White (or Liquid Clear, Liquid Black) makes
   every later color slide, blend and lighten by itself. Colors are never pre-mixed to the
   final value: the wet base and the brush do it. [S11E01 01:02, S12E10 00:06]
2. **Thin paint sticks to thick paint.** Base layers are dry and firm; later layers are thinned
   (Liquid White, Liquid Clear, thinner) so they stick on top. [S11E01 04:09, S20E09 21:16]
3. **Back to front.** Sky, then the farthest land, then each nearer plane, the foreground last.
   Each plane covers the bottom of the one behind it. [S11E01 25:19, S12E02 23:21]
4. **Dark first, light last.** Every form is laid in dark (or the base is already dark), then
   lit with a few touches. Darks are needed so the lights show. Never kill all the dark.
   [S11E01 18:34, S19E13 25:18, S17E07 22:16]
5. **One light.** Pick a side (usually the right) and keep it: highlights on faces turned to
   it, shadows on the others, all highlight strokes at one angle, shadows at the opposing one.
   Every highlight gets its own shadow. [S12E02 12:09, S12E13 7:21, S19E13 12:27]
6. **Angles.** Strokes follow the form: down the slope on mountains, along the rock face, along
   the lay of the land; straight down for reflections; dead level for water lines. Wrong
   angles show immediately. [S11E01 11:30, S07E04 12:04, S20E09 12:55]
7. **Mist between planes.** The base of every far mass is softened to haze with a clean dry
   brush (tap, lift); that separation is what makes depth. [S15E08 13:05, S12E02 7:26]
8. **Farther = lighter, bluer, softer, less detail.** Nearer = darker, warmer, sharper, more
   detail. [S07E04 15:20, S19E13 05:53, S11E01 23:57]
9. **Water lies flat and low.** Land is higher than water; water lines and ripples are
   perfectly horizontal; reflections fall straight down. [S11E01 19:32, S12E02 16:41, 21:14]
10. **Little paint, few strokes, no overworking.** Drop it in and leave it; blending too much
    gives a flat, lifeless sky and mud. [S07E04 01:47, S11E01 06:45]
11. **Don't over-mix.** Colors are often only half mixed on the knife or brush; the streaks are
    the texture. [S07E04 08:23, S12E10 09:19, S17E07 14:31]
12. **Clean, dry brushes for blending.** Wash in odorless thinner, shake, beat dry. A wet
    brush on wet paint makes mud. [S07E04 10:17, S12E02 5:01]

## What every painting has
- A **focal point**: usually a lit snowy peak (or a waterfall) near the center, slightly off.
- **Three planes** separated by mist: far (sky, mountain), middle (tree line, shore, water),
  near (big dark trees, banks, bushes).
- **Framing darks** at one or both edges: big dark trees or banks, often running off the canvas.
- **A light path into the picture**: open water, a stream or a path, light where it leads.
- **The lightest lights** (snow, cloud tops, water sheen) and **darkest darks** (evergreens,
  shaded banks) close to each other, near the focal point.
